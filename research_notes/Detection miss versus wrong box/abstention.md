# Objectness, open-world detection, and abstention

Scope: how a detector can say a region exists without committing it as a named object's location, and how evaluation can tell a wrong box from a miss and a miss from "not seen." Today is 27 October 2026. Papers before 2024 are marked older. No recommendation is made to raise or lower an overlap or size threshold; the sourced distinction is what to emit instead of a forced box.

## What class-agnostic objectness, unknown-object detectors, and SAM-style models claim to output, and what they refuse to name

### Takeaway
Objectness, as these papers define it, is a score that a region is some foreground thing, not a name for which thing. Segment Anything returns a valid mask and an estimated mask quality, and its data collection explicitly did not store names. Open-world detectors add a third label, "unknown," which is still a committed detection, distinct both from background and from a known class.

### Cited Findings
- Older (2021): ORE defines open-world detection as two duties: identify objects never introduced as training classes by labeling them unknown, with no explicit unknown supervision, and later learn those categories when labels arrive. The unknown label is the integer 0, separate from the known classes {1, …, C}. The base detector is Faster R-CNN, whose first stage is a class-agnostic region proposal network; the second stage classifies. ORE's unknown proposals are not human labels: an RPN auto-label marks proposals as pseudo-unknowns, and a Helmholtz free-energy head then separates known from unknown. Energy is fit with shifted Weibulls on a small held-out set that contains both known and unknown instances; a prediction is called unknown when the known-class energy is lower than the unknown energy. — [Joseph et al., CVPR 2021 / arXiv:2103.02603](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02603v2)
- Older (2021): standard detectors are trained to treat unlabeled objects as background. Joseph et al. state that instances of unknown classes "would be explicitly learned as background," and they cite Dhamija et al. that state-of-the-art detectors still emit those unknowns as known classes, often at high probability. — [Joseph et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02603v2)
- Older (2022): OW-DETR says ORE's RPN proposals "are likely to be biased to the known classes" because the RPN is trained with strong supervision from known classes. OW-DETR therefore does not treat RPN objectness as a name. It outputs three things from each query: a box, a novelty class (one of C known classes, the unknown class, or background), and a separate foreground objectness score whose job is to separate foreground (knowns plus pseudo-unknowns) from background so that "what constitutes a foreground object" can transfer from known classes to unknown ones. Pseudo-unknowns are the top-k unmatched queries with high activation inside their box on a channel-averaged backbone attention map. OW-DETR states it uses no held-out unknown supervision, unlike ORE's energy model. — [Gupta et al., CVPR 2022 / arXiv:2112.01513](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01513v1)
- Older (2022): OLN states the classification view of objectness ("how much does this region look like a foreground object?") and replaces it with a localization view ("how well does this region overlap with any ground-truth object?"). The score is geometric: centerness and IoU, trained with L1, with no foreground-versus-background classifier. At inference the box objectness is the geometric mean of centerness and predicted IoU; the mask variant adds mask-IoU as a third factor. OLN-Mask adds a class-agnostic mask head. The paper's claim is that a binary classifier overfits the labeled categories and treats unlabeled objects as background. On COCO cross-category (train on 20 VOC classes, recall only on 60 non-VOC classes, and do not spend the proposal budget on seen-class boxes), the abstract reports +3.3 AUC, +5.0 AR@10, and +5.1 AR@100 on novel categories versus prior proposal methods. The body reports OLN-Box at +4.9 AUC (+6.1 AR@10, +7.3 AR@100) over Faster R-CNN, and +3.3 AUC over the previous best refined RPNs. Cross-dataset: RoboNet +13 to +16 AP over the standard approach; Objects365 +4 AR@10 and +8 AR@100. Training samples anchors with IoU above 0.3 and does not explicitly sample background. — [Kim, Lin, Angelova, Kweon, and Kuo, arXiv:2108.06753](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.06753v1)
- Older (2023): PROB says standard detection classifies any proposal that misses a labeled object as background, so an unknown is trained as a negative. Its objectness head is not a class. Objectness is a multivariate Gaussian likelihood of the query embedding (Mahalanobis distance to a running mean and covariance of query embeddings). The final class distribution is the classification head's distribution multiplied by that objectness probability, so a near-zero objectness suppresses every class label, known or unknown. The classification head is then only asked to name the object conditional on it being an object. PROB reports that earlier OWOD methods sat near 10% unknown recall. — [Zohar, Wang, and Yeung, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Zohar_PROB_Probabilistic_Objectness_for_Open_World_Object_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- Older (2023): CAT's claim is that localizing and identifying in one decoder pass confuses known and unknown. The shared decoder runs twice: the first pass localizes foreground; the second pass identifies a known category or "unknown," using the location embedding as the class query. The training loss is the sum of three terms, localization, identification, and objectness. The paper cites Carrasco (2011) for the human-attention analogy and Kim et al. (OLN) for localization without a class. — [Ma et al., arXiv:2301.01970](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.01970v3)
- Older (2023): SAM's task is "return a valid segmentation mask given any prompt." "Valid" means that if the prompt is ambiguous (a point on a shirt could be the shirt or the person), the output must be a reasonable mask for at least one of those objects, not a class name. The model emits up to three masks per prompt and ranks them by a predicted IoU; the paper says three is enough for the usual nesting of whole, part, and subpart. The data engine "did not impose semantic constraints" and "did not collect these names or descriptions," even though annotators were asked to label things they could name. Automatic masks come from a 32×32 point grid, an IoU filter, a stability check (threshold the probability map at 0.5−δ and 0.5+δ and keep masks that agree), and NMS. SA-1B is over 1 billion masks on 11 million images. Given an image embedding, the prompt encoder and mask decoder run in about 50 ms in a browser. — [Kirillov et al., ICCV 2023 / arXiv:2304.02643](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02643)
- 2024: SOS states that vanilla SAM "is class-agnostic and does not classify the segments," and that it "does not exclusively segment objects" because prompts also return stuff and parts. SOS therefore puts a separate class-agnostic object prior (DINO self-attention, aggregated by elementwise max) in front of SAM, and treats that prior as "the probability of an image coordinate being part of any object," not a category. — [Wilms et al., arXiv:2409.14627](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14627v1)

### Inferences
- A candidate region in this literature is one of: an RPN or query box with an objectness or localization-quality score, a SAM mask with an estimated IoU, or a detection whose class slot is "unknown" or background. Only the last of these has entered the identity decision. Objectness and a mask are existence-without-a-name. The unknown class is a committed detection that refuses a known name but does not refuse being a detection.
- The lab's single vision-language call collapses the first and the third. OLN, CAT, PROB, and SAM all publish a second head or a second pass so that "a region is foreground" can be stored without writing it into a class slot.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary source that states a numeric "refusal rate" for SAM (how often it returns no mask). The paper specifies what a valid mask is, not an abstention rate.
- I did not open the original Alexe, Deselaers, and Ferrari "What is an object?" (2010) or the Faster R-CNN paper itself; RPN objectness is cited here only as later OWOD papers describe it.
- No 2025–2026 survey of class-agnostic proposal models was retrieved. SOS (2024) is the latest proposal-style source read in full.

## How selective prediction, conformal prediction, and reject-option classifiers abstain, what they guarantee, and what breaks for an uncalibrated vision-language score or a geometric resemblance score

### Takeaway
Abstention is a second output, "don't know," produced by thresholding a ranking on a held-out sample. The guarantee is about error among the cases that were not refused, or about a prediction set covering the truth, and it needs exchangeable labeled calibration data. It does not need the raw score to be a probability. An uncalibrated vision-language confidence and a geometric overlap score both fail that guarantee in published detection settings: the first because detection coverage stays below the nominal level, the second because a localization score is not a class probability and conformal box intervals, as published, cover only objects the detector already emitted.

### Cited Findings
- Older (2017): Geifman and El-Yaniv define a selective classifier as a pair (f, g). If g(x)=1 the output is f(x); if g(x)=0 the output is "don't know." Coverage is the probability that g(x)=1. Selective risk is the error of f only on the accepted region. They write that risk "can be traded-off for coverage," and that the whole profile is a risk-coverage curve. They do not assume the confidence function κ is a calibrated probability. They assume only that it ranks: a higher κ means the prediction is not less confident. Selection with Guaranteed Risk (SGR) binary-searches a threshold on κ using a held-out sample so that, for a user risk r* and a confidence δ, the selective risk is at most r* with probability at least 1−δ. They describe this as the reject option, a literature they date to about sixty years before 2017. Reported operating point: on ImageNet top-5, 2% error can be guaranteed with probability 99.9% at almost 60% test coverage, using VGG-16 or ResNet-50. On ImageNet, softmax response (the maximum softmax probability) beat MC-dropout as the ranking; on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 the two rankings were nearly identical. Softmax response is a threshold on the largest softmax entry, not a new class. — [Geifman and El-Yaniv, NeurIPS 2017 / arXiv:1705.08500](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.08500v2)
- Older (2022 revision; first posted 2020): Angelopoulos, Bates, Malik, and Jordan state that Platt scaling "attempt[s] to calibrate the network's probability estimates, but [does] not have formal guarantees." Their conformal sets contain the true ImageNet label with a user-chosen probability such as 90%, for every model and dataset, in finite samples. On ImageNet and ImageNet-V2 with ResNet-152, their sets were "often factors of 5 to 10 smaller than a stand-alone Platt scaling baseline." — [Angelopoulos et al., arXiv:2009.14193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14193)
- Older (2023 monograph): Angelopoulos and Bates state the split-conformal coverage claim used throughout this literature. For a calibration set of n fresh points and a user error rate α, the prediction set C satisfies 1−α ≤ P(Y_test ∈ C(X_test)) ≤ 1−α + 1/(n+1), when calibration and test points are exchangeable. They call this marginal coverage: the probability is averaged over the draw of the calibration set and the test point. The worked example uses softmax scores only as inputs to a calibration quantile, not as probabilities that are already correct. — [Angelopoulos and Bates, Foundations and Trends in Machine Learning, 2023](https://www.emerald.com/ftmal/article/16/4/494/1332423/Conformal-Prediction-A-Gentle-Introduction)
- 2024: Timans, Straehle, Sakmann, and Nalisnick (ECCV) build box intervals whose guarantee is class-conditional: the interval covers the true box with probability at least 1−α for every known class. They state two limits that matter here. First, distribution-free conditional coverage given the particular image is impossible; they cite that impossibility and settle for marginal, then class-conditional, coverage. The samples must be exchangeable. Second, "the provided guarantees only hold for true objects that are actually detected (true positives) and do not account for undetected objects (false negatives)." A box interval is also conditioned on the predicted class. Their two-step procedure first builds a conformal label set, then picks the box quantile from that set, so the box guarantee still applies when the top class is wrong. Naive one-step box conformal (Andéol et al., their cited prior) is valid only for correctly classified objects of a single class. Coordinate-wise conformal without a multiple-testing correction guarantees only 1−mα, where m is the number of box coordinates; Bonferroni restores 1−α and is conservative when coordinates are dependent. The nonconformity they calibrate is a geometric residual (absolute error, or absolute error divided by a scale), not the detector's classification confidence. — [Timans et al., ECCV 2024](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/12292.pdf)
- 2024: Tu et al. (ICML) report that vision-language models "are not inherently calibrated for uncertainty." Temperature scaling "significantly and consistently improves calibration," including when the test domain, label set, or hierarchy level differs from the calibration set, and a very small calibration set is enough. This is an empirical calibration result, not a finite-sample coverage guarantee. — [Tu et al., ICML 2024, PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tu24a.html)
- 2024, abstract only: Copley, Finlay, and Hiett say conformal scores on a YOLO detector can be arranged to give guarantees on "the percentage of objects which aren't detected" and on "the coverage of predicted bounding boxes" for aerial and satellite images. The full procedure was not read. — [Copley et al., COPA 2024, PMLR v230](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v230/copley24a.html)
- 2024, abstract only: Mukama et al. apply copula conformal regression to box residuals on KITTI and BDD100K so that the interval volume accounts for heteroscedastic, dependent coordinates. This paper tightens intervals; it is not an abstention rule. Full text was not read. — [Mukama et al., COPA 2024, PMLR v230](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v230/mukama24a.html)
- ICCV paper, year not printed in the extracted text: Liu and Mohanty apply split conformal abstention to CLIPSeg and Grounding DINO on KITTI, with no fine-tuning. The rule is: compute a conformity score on a calibration split; set q_α to the upper (1−α) quantile; return a prediction only if the test conformity is at most q_α; otherwise abstain. They state the usual exchangeability coverage claim. For segmentation it holds: CLIPSeg with test-time augmentation at α=0.05 covers 95.14% of pixels in normal weather and 95.45% in adverse weather, within 0.5 points of the nominal 95%. For detection it does not. A ground-truth object counts as covered only if some predicted box has IoU at least 0.5 and, unless they say otherwise, a matching class. Grounding DINO coverage "remains systematically below the target"; the best MC-dropout baseline "falls by nearly 10%." At α=0.10 under normal weather, their hybrid strategy reaches 69.47% coverage, against a 90% nominal level. Test-time augmentation "yield[s] zero valid predictions" for detection. Risk-coverage curves for detection "remain flat," and MC-dropout "lacks precision in abstention, often retaining low-quality boxes." They conclude that detection needs "task-aware abstention," and that a dense segmentation output admits plug-and-play conformal inference in a way detection does not. — [Liu and Mohanty, ICCV PDF](https://huazeliu.github.io/files/paper/ICCV_T2FM.pdf)
- 2025: Ye and Wei apply split conformal prediction to closed-ended visual question answering with large vision-language models, not to boxes. They report that a calibration quantile keeps empirical error below a user α on ScienceQA and MMMU across eight models, with no retraining and no prior on the answer distribution. Hallucinated answers at high confidence are the failure they target. This shows the same guarantee can be made for a vision-language label set when the output is a choice among known answers and calibration labels exist. It does not measure boxes. — [Ye and Wei, arXiv:2504.17671](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17671)

### Inferences
- SGR and conformal abstention both refuse to treat the score as a probability. They treat it as a rank, then spend labeled calibration points to pick a cutoff. If the lab's vision-language confidence is only a rank, the Geifman guarantee is still available for known identities, but only on a held-out sample drawn like the test sample, and only as a risk among committed answers. The refused answers are not negatives.
- A geometric resemblance (IoU, centerness, Mahalanobis distance to known-object embeddings) is the kind of score OLN and PROB use for objectness and the kind of residual Timans calibrates for box intervals. Published box conformal then guarantees that an interval around an already emitted box covers the true box. It does not guarantee that the emitted box was the right object, and Timans explicitly excludes misses. Using that geometric score as if it were a probability of identity has no theorem in the sources above.
- Liu and Mohanty are the direct evidence that an uncalibrated open-vocabulary detector confidence, even after MC-dropout or test-time augmentation and a conformal quantile, does not meet the nominal detection coverage. The same procedure does meet it for CLIPSeg's dense output. The break is the detection output (a set of boxes that must be matched), not the conformal algebra.
- Exchangeability is the assumption that fails if calibration frames and test frames are not interchangeable. Liu and Mohanty already show adverse weather moves detection coverage. Temporal dependence in a video is a further threat to that assumption; no paper read here measures it.

- Geifman's reference list was not opened, so the sixty-year reject-option source is not named here. Chow (1970) was not fetched.
- Copley et al. and Mukama et al. were not read beyond the PMLR abstracts. No numeric coverage from those two papers is reported.

## How open-world detectors treat unknown versus known objects, and how they score wilderness or unknown recall without counting a wrong identity as a true positive

### Takeaway
From 2021 onward the field splits "an unknown was called a known class" from "an unknown was retrieved." Wilderness impact and absolute open-set error penalize only the first. Unknown recall was added to measure the second. A later 2022 paper splits retrieval again into localization (a box landed on the unknown, even if the name is wrong) and discrimination (the name on that box is "unknown"). A wrong identity is an open-set error, not an unknown true positive.

### Cited Findings
- Older (2021): Joseph et al. evaluate known classes with mAP at IoU 0.5. For unknowns they refuse to use recall. They define wilderness impact as WI = P_K / P_{K∪U} − 1, with both precisions measured at recall 0.8. P_K is precision when only known classes are evaluated; P_{K∪U} is precision when known and unknown objects are both in the test set. WI is large when adding unknowns drops precision. Absolute open-set error (A-OSE), taken from Miller et al., is "the number count of unknown objects that get wrongly classified as any of the known class." They say both "implicitly measure how effective the model is in handling unknown objects." WI and A-OSE cannot be computed in their Task 4 because no unknown ground truth remains. On their Table 2, Task 1: Faster R-CNN WI 0.06991, A-OSE 13396, known mAP 56.16; ORE WI 0.02193, A-OSE 8234, known mAP 56.34; an oracle with all labels has WI 0.02004 and A-OSE 7080. Task 2, without their balanced finetuning, Faster R-CNN's mAP on the classes it already knew falls from 56.16 to 4.076. Finetuning restores previous-class mAP to 51.09 at WI 0.0375 and A-OSE 12497. ORE Task 2: WI 0.0154, A-OSE 7772, previous-known mAP 52.37, current-known 25.58, both 38.98. Task 3 ORE: WI 0.0081, A-OSE 6634, both-known mAP 29.32, against Faster R-CNN A-OSE 9174. The HTML table is flattened; these are the leading WI, A-OSE, and mAP figures in that order, and the 56.16 to 4.076 and 51.09 figures are also stated in the prose. — [Joseph et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02603v2)
- Older (2022): OW-DETR adds unknown recall (U-Recall), "a model's ability to retrieve the unknown object instances," reported beside known-class mAP. Standard Faster R-CNN and Deformable DETR have no U-Recall because they have no unknown output. Comparing against ORE without the energy-based unknown identifier (because that identifier uses a held-out unknown set), OW-DETR's Table 1 gives U-Recall 7.5, 6.2, and 5.7 on Tasks 1–3 against ORE−EBUI at 4.9, 2.9, and 3.9, and Task-1 known mAP 59.2 against 56.0. Absolute U-Recall gains over ORE are stated as 1.8 to 3.3 points. U-Recall is not computed in Task 4, when all 80 COCO classes are known. — [Gupta et al., 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.01513v1)
- Older (2022): Zhao et al. say prior OWOD metrics "simply focus on the unknown discrimination challenge from the perspective of known classes." They define two unknown-side metrics. Let TP_u be unknown ground truths correctly proposed as unknown, FN_u the unknown ground truths not recalled, and FN*_u the unknown ground truths recalled by a box that is misclassified. Then UDR = (TP_u + FN*_u) / (TP_u + FN_u), the rate at which unknowns are localized, including by a wrongly named box, and UDP = TP_u / (TP_u + FN*_u), the fraction of those localized unknowns that were actually labeled unknown. They say UDR and UDP "are complementary" and that "the primary concern of UDP and UDR is the accurate localization rather than classification." On their rebuilt benchmark the ORE row begins WI 0.04575, mAP 59.56, UR 7.76, UDR 20.39, UDP 38.04. The gap they emphasize is that UDR can be high while the unknown-class recall stays low: localization succeeded and the identity did not, and counting the misclassified box inside UDR is deliberate so that a method cannot look better at discrimination merely by emitting fewer boxes. Their Proposal Advisor is what moves UDR; the Class-specific Expelling Classifier is what pushes misclassified unknowns off the known-class decision. — [Zhao et al., arXiv:2201.00471](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.00471v2)
- Older (2023): PROB's Table 1 on the superclass-mixed benchmark (M-OWODB), U-Recall then Task-1 current-known mAP: OCPL 8.26 and 56.6; 2B-OCD 12.1 and 56.4; OW-DETR 7.5 and 59.2; PROB 19.4 and 59.5. PROB's later-task U-Recall is 17.4 and 19.6. The abstract's summary is about 2× unknown recall and about 10% higher known mAP than prior OWOD methods; the body says 2–3× and "relative gains of 100–300%." PROB also reports A-OSE 5195, 6452, and 2641 on Tasks 1–3 against OW-DETR's 10240, 8441, and 6803, so the extra unknown recall is not explained as extra known-class false positives. PROB refuses ORE's energy identifier for the same reason OW-DETR does: it uses a held-out set of unknown images. On the superclass-separated split, unknown recall is harder because the tasks do not share a super-category (generalizing from animals to vehicles, rather than from dogs to cats). An ablation that drops objectness likelihood-maximization makes objectness "random": background patches are predicted as unknowns, and known mAP falls. Replacing Mahalanobis distance with L2 (equivalent to a spherical Gaussian at the origin) is also worse. 2B-OCD, which grafts OLN's localization objectness onto an ORE-style detector, is the intermediate point: PROB says Kim et al.'s localization objectness is worth about 4 U-Recall points from OW-DETR to 2B-OCD, and the table shows 12.1 versus 7.5 on Task 1. — [Zohar et al., 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Zohar_PROB_Probabilistic_Objectness_for_Open_World_Object_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- Older (2023): CAT, on the OWOD split, reports U-Recall 21.8, 19.2, and 24.4 on Tasks 1–3 against OW-DETR's 7.5, 6.2, and 5.7, "absolute gains up to 18.7%." On their MS-COCO split, which they say reduces leakage across tasks, CAT reports 24.0, 23.0, and 24.6 against OW-DETR's 5.7, 6.2, and 6.9, gains up to 18.3 points, and known-class mAP gains up to 9.7 points. Removing cascade decoding drops unknown recall by 2.0, 2.4, and 2.6 points and known mAP by 0.6, 2.3, 4.0, and 3.1 points across the four tasks. Removing the self-adaptive pseudo-label rule drops unknown recall by 4.6, 5.0, and 7.6 points. CAT still reports WI and A-OSE beside U-Recall and says the first two "implicitly quantify" unknown handling while U-Recall measures retrieval. — [Ma et al., arXiv:2301.01970](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.01970v3)

### Inferences
- A wrong identity is not an unknown true positive in this protocol. A-OSE counts it. U-Recall, as OW-DETR, PROB, and CAT use it, is retrieval of the unknown instance as unknown; Zhao et al. then show that a single recall number still mixes two events, and they put the wrongly named but overlapping box in UDR's numerator and in UDP's denominator. That is the published separation between "something is here" and "this is that object."
- WI and A-OSE do not reward emitting an unknown box, and they do not punish silence. A-OSE rises only when an unknown ground truth is classified as a known class. WI rises when the presence of unknowns lowers precision at a fixed recall of 0.8. A system that drops every doubtful box can therefore improve WI and A-OSE while U-Recall and UDR fall. That is the same tradeoff as dropping a doubtful part versus keeping a box on the wrong area.
- "Unknown" is not "absent," and "background" is not "absent" either. Background is the negative class the detector was trained to assign to anything without a known label (Joseph, PROB). Unknown is a positive output that a later task is allowed to rename. Neither paper treats a missing box as a recorded observation that the frame was searched.

### Gaps
- The exact IoU and label rule inside OW-DETR's U-Recall (whether IoU must exceed 0.5, and whether a known-class box on an unknown object counts) is not stated in the passages retrieved. Zhao et al. are the source that defines the misclassified-but-localized case explicitly. Later-task UR/UDR/UDP numbers from Zhao's flattened HTML table are not quoted beyond the first task, because the column alignment after the first five entries is ambiguous.
- No 2024–2026 open-world detection survey with a new comparative table was retrieved. The newest OWOD system read end to end is CAT (2023). SOS (2024) is open-world instance segmentation, not a successor to the ORE benchmark.
- Miller et al. and Dhamija et al. are cited by Joseph for A-OSE and for high-confidence open-set false positives; those papers were not re-read.

## Which metrics punish a wrong box differently from a miss, and which one shows the lab's tradeoff

### Takeaway
Average precision at a single overlap threshold gives a full true positive to a box that barely qualifies, and it folds the score threshold into one curve, so a wrong region and a dropped region are not visible as different decisions. PDQ and HOTA's detection Jaccard charge a wrong box as its own error and still charge the miss. TETA charges localization inside a local cluster and charges the name separately, so a right place with a wrong name is not a total failure. The honest report of the lab's tradeoff is the pair (false positives from committed boxes, false negatives from withheld boxes), plus a localization quality that does not saturate at one overlap cutoff, plus an identity term that is not that localization term.

### Cited Findings
- Older (2020): Hall et al. define probabilistic object detection as emitting a spatial distribution and a label distribution, and PDQ as the metric. Pairwise PDQ is the geometric mean of spatial quality Q_S and label quality Q_L, so either factor at zero zeros the pair. Q_S is 1 only when the detector puts spatial probability 1 on every ground-truth pixel and 0 outside; it is exp(−(foreground loss + background loss)), with foreground loss the average negative log probability on the object pixels. Q_L is the probability the detector assigned to the ground-truth class, whether or not that class was the top class. Assignment is Hungarian on pairwise PDQ, not a greedy pass over label confidence. The dataset score is the sum of matched pairwise PDQ divided by the count of true positives, false negatives, and false positives. An unmatched detection and a missed ground truth contribute nothing to the numerator and still increment the denominator. There is no overlap threshold that turns a detection into a full success. Their Figure 3: a box that covers 16% of an airplane's pixels is a completely correct detection under AP at IoU 0.5, and has pairwise PDQ 3.64×10^−6 if the detector reports no spatial uncertainty. They conclude that conventional detectors are spatially overconfident and score poorly as probabilistic detectors. A non-probabilistic box can be scored by setting probability 1−ε inside the box and ε outside. They also note AP averages the maximum precision at several recall levels and uses a fixed IoU, typically 0.5, as the definition of a true positive. — [Hall et al., WACV 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_WACV_2020/papers/Hall_Probabilistic_Object_Detection_Definition_and_Evaluation_WACV_2020_paper.pdf)
- Older (2021, IJCV): Luiten et al. define HOTA so that detection, association, and localization are separate. Matching is one-to-one per frame and only if spatial similarity is at least a threshold α. A matched pair is a true positive. An unmatched ground truth is a false negative (a miss). An unmatched prediction is a false positive (an extra box). Detection accuracy at that α is the Jaccard index |TP| / (|TP| + |FN| + |FP|). HOTA averages that score across localization thresholds, and a separate LocA averages the spatial similarity of the true positives across those same thresholds. They list five error types from Leichter and Krupka: false negatives, false positives, fragmentations, mergers, and deviations, corresponding to detection recall, detection precision, association recall, association precision, and localization. — [Luiten et al., International Journal of Computer Vision, 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-020-01375-2)
- Older (2022): Li et al. show why a class-grouped metric hides the distinction the lab needs. On one clip, tracker A follows the object and is slightly wrong at a fine-grained class; tracker B names it correctly and does not track it. mMOTA scores A at 0 and B at 33. They ask whether a trajectory is still meaningful when the class is wrong, and answer yes: a van tracked as a bus is still a usable trajectory for planning. TETA therefore has three scores, combined by an arithmetic mean because a geometric mean would be zero whenever classification is zero, which is common in a long tail. Localization accuracy is |TPL| / (|TPL| + |FPL| + |FNL|), computed inside a local cluster around each ground-truth box. Predictions are assigned to the nearest ground truth within an IoU margin r. Classification is scored only on matches with localization threshold at least 0.5, and only then. Association does not require the class to match: their tracker associates every neighboring detection (Associate Every Thing) and uses class exemplars as a soft cue rather than as a hard gate. For incomplete labels, predictions that fall in no cluster are ignored rather than counted as false positives, because TAO-style datasets are not exhaustively annotated and treating every unmatched box as a false positive punishes true objects that were never labeled. They report that at r of 0.7 or higher, the chance of two ground-truth boxes colliding in one cluster is under 1% even on crowded MOT20. With exhaustive single-class labels the classification term is meaningless, r can be 0, and TETA then differs from HOTA mainly by using an arithmetic mean. — [Li, Danelljan, Ding, Huang, and Yu, ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820486.pdf)
- Older (2022), supplementary: if annotations are exhaustive, unmatched predictions inside a cluster remain false positives, and predictions in no cluster are classification false positives (background called foreground). The class-agnostic alternative, they say, would be dominated by common classes on a long-tailed set, which is why they keep per-class scoring but select the predictions by location rather than by the predicted label. — [Li et al., supplementary, arXiv:2207.12978](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.12978)

### Inferences
- Under HOTA's Jaccard, a committed box that misses the localization threshold adds one false positive and leaves the ground truth as a false negative. Dropping that box removes the false positive and leaves the false negative. The wrong box is the more expensive event. A single average precision, or a single operating point chosen by a confidence threshold, does not show those two counts. PDQ makes the same split inside the denominator, and in addition refuses to give the barely-overlapping box a full true positive: the 16% airplane is essentially a zero, not a success.
- TETA is the metric that refuses to score a wrong name as a missed object. Localization is matched by place. The name is a third number. For the lab, a box on the wrong area lowers LocA (it is a false positive localization, and the part remains a false negative localization). A box on the part with the wrong identity lowers only the classification term. A withheld box lowers localization recall and does not create a false positive. Those three outcomes are one tradeoff, and TETA prints them as three numbers. HOTA and mAP, grouped by class, collapse the first and the second into a miss.
- The caveat from the same paper: if the label set is incomplete, TETA ignores boxes that overlap no annotated object. A wrong-area box on an unfamiliar part that was never boxed in the ground truth would not be punished. PDQ and HOTA, which treat every unmatched prediction as a false positive, punish it. Which of those is "honest" depends on whether the frame is exhaustively labeled. Hall et al. require a mask, or they fill the box with a uniform probability; they are explicit that a box-only ground truth is an approximation.
- None of these metrics has a state for "this frame was processed and this object was not committed." A miss is a false negative. Abstention, in Geifman and in Liu and Mohanty, removes the case from the answered set and reports coverage separately. That coverage number is the missing state: seen, not committed, not declared absent.

### Gaps
- No primary source defining a metric named OW-TETA was found. The open-world tracking pointer inside Li et al. is their citation of Liu et al. for a recall-based open-world MOT evaluation; that paper was not opened. TETA itself is the metric that was read.
- PDQ's numeric detector leaderboard (which network scored what) was not extracted. The airplane example and the aggregation formula were.
- HOTA's published MOTChallenge numbers were not extracted. The definitions were.

## The published result that one score threshold cannot raise both precision and recall, and the second output used instead

### Takeaway
For a fixed ranking, a threshold picks one point on a curve that trades precision against recall, or risk against coverage. The papers that move both numbers do it by emitting something other than a harder or softer version of the same box: an abstention, a class-agnostic mask or localization score, a cascade that localizes before it names, or a track that is scored apart from the class.

### Cited Findings
- Older (2017): Geifman and El-Yaniv state the tradeoff directly: "the risk of a selective classifier can be traded-off for coverage." SGR's only degree of freedom is the threshold on one confidence function. Meeting a stricter risk lowers coverage. The second output is g(x)=0, "don't know," and the risk is computed only on the complement. Their ImageNet point is the numerical form of the tradeoff: 2% top-5 error at 99.9% confidence is available at almost 60% coverage, not at full coverage. They also note that a cost-based reject rule needs a cost for abstaining and a cost for being wrong, and that those costs are often unavailable; SGR takes a target risk instead of a cost. — [Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.08500v2)
- Older (2020): Hall et al. define AP as the average of maximum precision across recall levels, after a fixed IoU rule has already turned each detection into a binary success or failure. One score threshold is one recall level. Their empirical point is sharper than the curve: at IoU 0.5 the 16%-of-the-airplane box is a true positive, so thresholding the confidence cannot express that the box is spatially wrong. The second output they require is a spatial probability per pixel and a probability on the true class, combined by a geometric mean with no success threshold. — [Hall et al., 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_WACV_2020/papers/Hall_Probabilistic_Object_Detection_Definition_and_Evaluation_WACV_2020_paper.pdf)
- Older (2022): OLN's ablation claim is that swapping the loss on the same classifier head is not enough: an RPN trained with an L1 regression of a 0/1 target "does not help," whereas deleting the classifier and predicting centerness and IoU does. The second output is that localization-quality score, and optionally a class-agnostic mask. Novel-category AR moves by the amounts cited above (+5.0 AR@10 and +5.1 AR@100 in the abstract). A threshold on a foreground classifier cannot recover those boxes, because the classifier was trained to call them background. — [Kim et al., 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.06753v1)
- Older (2022): Li et al.'s mMOTA example is a published case where no threshold on a class score repairs both failures. Tracker A's wrong fine-grained label zeros the class-grouped metric even though the track is perfect; tracker B's correct label scores 33 with no track. The second output is the track, associated without requiring the class to agree, and the metric then reports LocA, association, and classification as three numbers. Their arithmetic mean exists so that a zero classification score does not wipe out a correct track. — [Li et al., 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820486.pdf)
- Older (2023): CAT's ablation is the detection version of the same claim. One decoding pass that localizes and identifies together loses both known mAP and unknown recall relative to two passes. The second output is the location embedding, produced before the class embedding and then reused as the class query. The measured drops from removing that split are the 0.6 to 4.0 mAP points and the 2.0 to 2.6 U-Recall points cited above. — [Ma et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.01970v3)
- Older (2023): PROB multiplies objectness into the class distribution instead of thresholding one classification score. Disabling the objectness likelihood step does not slide along a better precision-recall point; it makes objectness random, predicts background as unknown, and "drastically" reduces known mAP. The second output is the objectness probability, trained as a density, not a lower threshold on the class head. — [Zohar et al., 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Zohar_PROB_Probabilistic_Objectness_for_Open_World_Object_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- 2024: Liu and Mohanty's detection risk-coverage curves "remain flat," which is the empirical statement that sweeping one conformity threshold on Grounding DINO does not buy higher coverage at acceptable risk. Their hybrid run at α=0.10 still sits at 69.47% coverage. Segmentation, where the second structure is a dense map rather than a matched box, does track the nominal coverage. — [Liu and Mohanty, ICCV PDF](https://huazeliu.github.io/files/paper/ICCV_T2FM.pdf)

### Inferences
- The result the lab can use without touching its overlap or size gates: a single confidence, whether a vision-language score or a geometric resemblance, selects an operating point. Publishing a candidate (objectness, a mask, an unknown box, or an explicit don't-know with the frame marked seen) is how the papers above record the other axis. Committing the doubtful region as the object's box spends the false-positive term in HOTA, TETA's localization Jaccard, PDQ's denominator, and A-OSE if the name is a known class. Dropping it spends the false-negative term and, under WI and A-OSE alone, can look like an improvement.
- "This frame was seen and this object was not committed" is Geifman's don't-know, or Liu and Mohanty's abstention when conformity exceeds the calibration quantile, together with a coverage count. It is not Joseph's background class, and it is not a negative A-OSE. Background and a miss both look like absence in a detection list. Coverage is the quantity that keeps them apart.

### Gaps
- Davis and Goadrich (ICML 2006) on the precision-recall curve versus ROC was not fetched. The threshold tradeoff is cited here from Geifman and El-Yaniv's risk-coverage statement and from Hall et al.'s definition of AP, not from that paper.
- No theorem was found that says a second head always dominates a threshold on the same score. The empirical comparisons above each add a different second output, on different datasets.
- A 2026 language-model preprint was seen that claims an adaptive, per-context threshold raised both precision and recall relative to one static threshold. It is not a detection result and was not used.
