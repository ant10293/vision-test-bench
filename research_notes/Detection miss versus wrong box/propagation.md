# Propagation without redrawing a box every frame

Scope: how promptable video object segmentation and tracking-by-detection keep a location after a sparse confirmation, where that propagation still lands on the wrong region, and what can run locally without a closed label set. Date of this note: 27 October 2026. Papers from 2022–2023 are marked as older. No claim below is a recommendation to add another threshold on the current vision-language box.

Benchmarks named below, so later numbers are readable: semi-supervised video object segmentation (VOS) is given a first-frame mask and scored by region similarity \(\mathcal{J}\), contour accuracy \(\mathcal{F}\), and their mean \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\). YouTube-VOS reports \(\mathcal{G}\), the average of seen and unseen \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\). DAVIS 2017 is a short, densely annotated VOS set. MOSE is a crowded, heavily occluded VOS set. LVOS / LVOS v2 are long-video VOS sets. SA-V is Meta’s open-world “any object or part” video set. BURST is an open-world video segmentation set scored by open-world tracking accuracy (OWTA) on common vs uncommon classes. MOT17/MOT20 are closed-set pedestrian tracking. DanceTrack is closed-set human tracking with similar appearance, occlusion, and non-linear motion. TAO is a large-vocabulary tracking set. TETA splits tracking into localization (LocA), association (AssocA), and classification (ClsA).

## How do SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, DEVA, and similar systems propagate a mask from a sparse set of confirmed frames, and what prompt do they require?

### Takeaway

These systems treat one confirmed frame as the identity and later frames as memory readout. SAM 2 can be prompted with a point, a box, or a mask on any frame and then streams a masklet; XMem and Cutie, in the standard protocol, require a first-frame mask. DEVA is the architecture that keeps an image segmenter and a class-agnostic propagator separate, and it only merges a new image hypothesis into an existing track when mask IoU exceeds 0.5.

### Cited Findings

- SAM 2’s promptable visual segmentation task takes positive or negative clicks, boxes, or masks on any frame. A prompt must produce a valid mask on that frame immediately. After the initial prompts, the model propagates a spatio-temporal masklet to every frame. Further prompts on any frame refine the masklet. Prompts may come from frames that are in the future relative to the frame being decoded. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2, arXiv:2408.00714](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- The official video API is `add_new_points_or_box` (points and/or a box on a frame and object id) followed by `propagate_in_video`. The released code and checkpoints are public; the paper states the training code is Apache 2.0. The HTML version of the paper says its reported results are the improved checkpoint that the GitHub repository labels SAM 2.1. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2); [facebookresearch/sam2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2/)
- Propagation is not a second detection. An image encoder runs once per frame. Memory attention conditions that embedding on a memory bank. A lightweight mask decoder then emits the mask. The memory encoder fuses the predicted mask with the frame embedding and stores it for later frames. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- The memory bank is a FIFO of up to \(N\) recent-frame spatial memories plus a FIFO of up to \(M\) prompted-frame memories. In the standard VOS setting the only prompt is the initial mask, and the bank keeps that first frame together with up to \(N\) recent unprompted frames. Object-pointer vectors from the mask decoder are stored as a high-level semantic summary and are cross-attended alongside the spatial memories. Temporal position is embedded only into the \(N\) recent frames, not into prompted frames, because prompted frames can lie far from the temporal range seen in training. The paper describes \(N\) and \(M\) symbolically; this note did not find the integer defaults in the paper text. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- Training prompts are mixed: ground-truth mask with probability 0.5, a positive click with probability 0.25, or a box with probability 0.25, on sequences of 8 frames with up to 2 frames prompted. There is an occlusion head: an extra token and MLP score whether the object is visible. Frames predicted invisible receive a learned occlusion embedding in memory. Memory attention uses \(L=4\) layers by default. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- SAM 2 tracks multiple objects separately. They share per-frame image embeddings and do not communicate with each other. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- During the authors’ own data collection, Phase 1 had no temporal model: annotators used image SAM at 6 fps and spent 37.8 seconds per frame. Phase 2 propagated mask prompts only (4.5–7.4 s/frame depending on phase; Phase 2 was 7.4 s/frame, about 5.1× Phase 1). Phase 3 allowed point and mask refinement and reached 4.5 s/frame, about 8.4× faster than per-frame annotation. SA-V has 50.9K videos and 642.6K masklets; more than 88% of SA-V masks have normalized area under 0.1; the disappearance-and-reappearance rate on SA-V Manual is 42.5%. The val/test targets were chosen to be fast, occluded, and disappearing, and were annotated at 6 fps without the SAM 2 tracker, to avoid evaluating the model on its own propagation. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- XMem (ECCV 2022, older) is semi-supervised VOS: the user provides a first-frame annotation, and the model segments the other frames online. It does not take a box or a point as the published task input; the memory is initialized from the image plus the mask. Three stores are read on every new frame: a GRU sensory memory updated every frame, a high-resolution working memory updated every \(r\)-th frame, and a compact long-term memory of prototypes. Readout is attention from the query frame onto keys and values in working and long-term memory. The first frame is protected from consolidation: when working memory is full, the ground-truth frame and the most recent \(T_{\min}-1\) memory frames stay at high resolution. — [Cheng and Schwing, XMem, arXiv:2207.07115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07115)
- XMem’s stated failure of short memory: recurrent and frame-to-frame methods “lack long-term context and often lose track after object occlusions.” A single growing attention memory, the prior pattern, becomes impractical past roughly a minute of video on consumer GPUs. With a 6 GB budget the authors say XMem can reach about 34,000 frames before memory pressure; beyond a cap, least-frequently-used long-term prototypes are evicted. — [Cheng and Schwing, XMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07115)
- Cutie (2023, older relative to 2026) uses the same semi-supervised contract: a first-frame segmentation, then streaming frames. It keeps XMem’s pixel memory and adds an object memory: a small set of object queries, a query-based object transformer, and foreground-background masked attention so some queries see only the foreground and others only the background. Pixel memory uses top-\(k\) filtering with \(k=30\). For videos of more than about a thousand frames (BURST, LVOS) it swaps in XMem’s long-term memory with no retraining. Inference is online and the compute per frame does not grow with video length. — [Cheng et al., Cutie, arXiv:2310.12982](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)
- The Cutie repository also ships an interactive GUI whose “permanent memory,” inherited from XMem++, can pin user-committed frames. That is an extra control on top of the paper’s first-frame protocol, not a different task. — [hkchengrex/Cutie](https://github.com/hkchengrex/Cutie/)
- DEVA (ICCV 2023, older) splits the problem in two. A task-specific image segmenter proposes masks. A class-agnostic temporal model, trained once on mask-propagation data, carries those masks. The authors explicitly contrast this with tracking-by-detection, where the image boxes are treated as immutable and the tracker only associates them. DEVA instead denoises image masks by in-clip consensus and merges them with forward propagation. — [Cheng et al., DEVA, ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- DEVA’s propagator `Prop(H, I)` takes a memory of already segmented frames and paints those objects onto a query frame. In-clip consensus aligns a short future clip (default clip length \(n=3\) in the semi-online setting, \(n=1\) online) onto the current frame with a temporary one-frame memory that is discarded immediately. A proposal supports another if IoU \(> 0.5\); selected proposals may not overlap each other above that same 0.5. Lone proposals are treated as noise. — [Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- Merging propagation with a new image consensus is bipartite matching at a minimum IoU of 0.5. Matched pairs are fused. Unmatched segments from either side pass through; they are not discarded on a single miss. A propagated segment is deleted only after it fails to match any consensus segment for \(L=5\) consecutive times. By default the image model is merged every 5 frames. — [Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- DEVA’s demonstrated image front-ends include SAM with no user prompt (a point grid), and Grounding DINO plus SAM for a text prompt such as “beyblade.” The authors’ code note of 12 Sep 2023 says automatic mode queries 64 points per side and skips points already inside a segment, in order to run faster and catch smaller objects. No finetuning is required to swap the image model. — [Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf); [hkchengrex/Tracking-Anything-with-DEVA](https://github.com/hkchengrex/Tracking-Anything-with-DEVA)
- When the same methods are scored as classical first-frame-mask VOS, the SAM 2 paper’s Table 6 gives \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) (or \(\mathcal{G}\) on YouTube-VOS 2019) as MOSE val / DAVIS 2017 val / LVOS val / SA-V val / SA-V test / YouTube-VOS 2019: XMem 59.6 / 86.0 / — / 60.1 / 62.3 / 85.6; DEVA 66.0 / 87.0 / 55.9 / 55.4 / 56.2 / 85.4; Cutie-base 69.9 / 87.9 / 66.0 / 60.7 / 62.7 / 87.0; SAM 2 Hiera-B+ 76.6 / 90.2 / 78.0 / 76.8 / 77.0 / 88.6; SAM 2 Hiera-L 77.9 / 90.7 / 78.0 / 77.9 / 78.4 / 89.3. Prior methods cluster near 60–63 on SA-V val/test; SAM 2 Hiera-L is 77.9 / 78.4. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- Cutie’s own Table 1, re-run on the authors’ hardware with ImageNet-only pretraining, does not match those SAM 2-paper numbers. Trained with MOSE, Cutie-base is 68.3 \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) on MOSE (64.2 \(\mathcal{J}\), 72.3 \(\mathcal{F}\)), 88.8 on DAVIS 2017 val, 85.3 on DAVIS 2017 test-dev, and 86.5 \(\mathcal{G}\) on YouTube-VOS 2019. The same table’s XMem trained with MOSE is 59.6 on MOSE and 86.0 on DAVIS 2017 val; XMem trained without MOSE is 56.3 on MOSE and 86.2 on DAVIS. DEVA in that table is 66.0 on MOSE when trained with MOSE and 60.0 without. — [Cheng et al., Cutie, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)
- The SAM 2 GitHub checkpoint table (measurements dated with the 29 Sep 2024 SAM 2.1 release in that README) again differs: sam2.1 Hiera-L is 224.4M parameters, 39.5 FPS, SA-V test \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) 79.5, MOSE val 74.6, LVOS v2 80.6. The July 2024 sam2 Hiera-L line is 39.7 FPS, SA-V test 76.0, MOSE val 74.6, LVOS v2 79.8. Tiny through large SAM 2.1 sizes are 38.9M / 46M / 80.8M / 224.4M at 91.2 / 84.8 / 64.1 / 39.5 FPS. — [facebookresearch/sam2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2/)

### Inferences

- A confirmed box is a legal SAM 2 prompt, but the thing that moves forward is the mask written into memory, not the box. XMem and Cutie want that mask up front. A practical sparse-confirmation path is: turn a confirmed box or point into a mask once, then call the propagator. That is how DEVA already uses SAM.
- DEVA is the closest published split to “discover rarely, propagate otherwise.” The image model can be absent for four frames out of five. A missed image call does not, by itself, delete the object or require a new box; deletion waits for five consecutive failed IoU matches. A new image segment that does not match is a new object, not a repair of the old one.
- The three published SAM 2 score tables (paper Table 6, GitHub 2.1, Cutie’s re-run of older models) are not interchangeable. Report the table with the claim. The qualitative ordering is stable: first-frame-mask propagation beats per-frame open-world segmentation on SA-V, and MOSE remains much harder than DAVIS.

### Gaps

- Integer defaults for SAM 2’s \(N\) and \(M\) were not in the paper text fetched here. Do not cite a memory length of 7 unless a config file is quoted.
- No primary source in this pass states that XMem or Cutie accept a raw box or point without a mask. The Cutie GUI permanent-memory path was not re-read from the paper.
- This pass did not find a published evaluation of SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, or DEVA when the only available frames are 1 Hz apart. Cutie notes that DAVIS is 24 fps and YouTube-VOS is 30 fps but annotated at 6 fps, and the authors re-ran methods at full frame rate. Accuracy at a one-second gap is unevidenced.

## What published failure modes put the mask on a neighboring object, especially with similar appearance, hand occlusion, or camera motion?

### Takeaway

The documented wrong-object failures are distractor pixel-matching, greedy commitment of an uncertain mask into memory, and independent per-object decoding with no exclusion between neighbors. Hand occlusion does not have its own numeric rate in the SAM 2 / Cutie / XMem / DEVA papers fetched here. The quantitative signature of these failures is a large \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) drop from DAVIS to MOSE or SA-V, not a published “percent of masks on the wrong part.”

### Cited Findings

- SAM 2’s own limitations: it can fail across shot changes; it can lose or confuse objects in crowds, after long occlusions, and in long videos; it struggles with thin, fast detail; nearby objects of similar appearance are a distinct failure, with the example “multiple identical juggling balls.” The authors’ suggested mitigation inside the product is another prompt on a later frame, not a better automatic guess. They also say more explicit motion modeling could reduce the similar-object errors. Because objects are decoded independently, nothing stops two masklets from claiming the same neighborhood. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- Meta’s SAM 2 write-up repeats the same limits in product language: loss across drastic camera viewpoint changes, long occlusions, crowds, and long videos; confusion among similar-looking objects when only one frame is prompted (their example is horses); refinement prompts on later frames often repair it. They also name camera motion, blur, and low resolution as reasons video is harder than images. — [Meta, Segment Anything 2](https://ai.meta.com/blog/segment-anything-2/)
- SAM2Long (arXiv:2410.16268, Oct 2024) states the mechanism of the wrong-region commit. The decoder emits three masks, each with a predicted IoU, plus one occlusion score \(o_t\): \(o_t>0\) means present, \(o_t<0\) means absent, and \(|o_t|\) is confidence. SAM 2 then keeps only the highest-IoU mask. On easy frames that is fine. On occlusion or reappearance the proposals are uncertain, but the same greedy rule still writes one mask into memory. Once that mask is in the FIFO, later frames attend to it and the error is not undone. Their qualitative example is a dancer: after occlusion, SAM 2 switches to a different person. A second failure, which their method also misses, is a dynamic background where the model tracks the wrong shirt or loses the target; they attribute that to reliance on fine-grained visual detail and a lack of semantic identity. — [Ding et al., SAM2Long](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16268v3)
- SAM2Long’s measured cost of that commit, on their reproduction: SAM2Long-L gains 5.3 \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) on SA-V test and the large model gains 4.5 on SA-V val over same-size SAM 2; SAM2Long-S gains 4.7 on SA-V val and 3.5 on LVOS v2 val over SAM 2-S. Across the 12 size/dataset comparisons they summarize, the average gain is 3.7 \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\). Gains are larger on longer videos. The method is training-free. It keeps several memory pathways, refuses to store frames whose occlusion or IoU says the object is absent or poorly segmented, and, when every pathway is uncertain, forces the pathways to keep distinct masks. The image encoder is still run once (SAM 2-Large is 224M total, of which 212M is the image encoder), so the extra decoder passes are the added cost. These deltas are relative to the authors’ reproduction, not to every SAM 2 table above. — [Ding et al., SAM2Long](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16268v3)
- Cutie’s diagnosis of the neighbor-object error is older and specific: pixel-level memory reading matches each query pixel independently to memory pixels, so it has no object-level consistency. In the presence of distractors the match is noisy. They state that recent methods (XMem and AOT) lose more than 20 \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) when moved from DAVIS 2017 to MOSE. Their Table 1 makes that concrete for models trained without MOSE: XMem 86.2 on DAVIS 2017 val vs 56.3 on MOSE (a 29.9 point drop); AOT-R50 84.9 vs 58.4. Cutie-base trained without MOSE reaches 64.0 on MOSE, 8.7 above that XMem 56.3, which is the abstract’s “+8.7 over XMem.” With MOSE in training the gap shrinks but does not vanish: Cutie-base 68.3 vs XMem 59.6. Adding BURST and OVIS (“MEGA”) is another +1.6 on MOSE. They also say YouTube-VOS and DAVIS training became too easy for their model, above 93% IoU during training, which is why MOSE was needed. — [Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)
- MOSE itself, as described by a 2024 challenge report citing Ding et al. 2023: 2,149 clips, 5,200 objects, 36 categories, 431,725 masks; targets are crowded, occluded, and disappear. LVOS, cited there as Hong et al.: 220 videos, 421 minutes, with long-term reappearance and similar objects across time. — [LSVOS challenge VOS report, arXiv:2408.10125](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10125v1)
- A Sep 2025 LSVOS/MOSEv2 report states that SAM 2 “lacks explicit object tracking mechanisms” and so “does not guarantee identity consistency in multi-object or long-occlusion scenarios.” Their motion-prediction module keeps a per-object kinematic state (location, size, velocity) and, under occlusion, adds a Gaussian spatial prior to the segmentation logits. They say this is aimed at short disappearances and at confusion among visually similar instances. The prior is scaled to the predicted box so small objects get a tighter Gaussian. They do not publish a standalone ablation number for “percent switched to the neighbor” in the text fetched here. — [SCOPE / MOSEv2, arXiv:2509.15781](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15781v1)
- DEVA’s authors list propagation-specific false-positive modes on the repository: positive detections are amplified across time, so a lower false-positive image model (a higher detection threshold in their words) helps; scenes where objects constantly enter and leave fill the memory and raise the false-positive rate. A Mar 2024 code fix was required because unmatched segments were not being deleted, and noisy detections accumulated on long videos. On closed-set data with enough video labels they say end-to-end models are still stronger. — [hkchengrex/Tracking-Anything-with-DEVA](https://github.com/hkchengrex/Tracking-Anything-with-DEVA)
- DEVA’s quantitative open-world result is not a failure rate, but it shows how much class-agnostic propagation beats per-frame association on uncommon objects. On BURST, Mask2Former plus a box tracker scores OWTA 60.9 / 66.9 / 24.0 (all / common / uncommon) on val; Mask2Former plus STCN 64.6 / 71.0 / 25.0; OWTB 55.8 / 59.8 / 38.8. DEVA semi-online with Mask2Former reaches 69.9 / 75.2 / 41.5, and with EntitySeg (class-agnostic entity segmentation, no class labels) 69.5 / 73.3 / 50.5. Uncommon-class OWTA is where the class-agnostic image model wins. False-positive tracks are not directly penalized, because BURST annotations are not exhaustive. — [Cheng et al., DEVA, Table 2](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- XMem (2022) states the short-horizon failure without a neighbor-switch rate: sensory memory “fails for long-term prediction due to representation drift”; tracking-style propagation “often lose[s] track after object occlusions.” — [Cheng and Schwing, XMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07115)
- Egocentric and hand-occlusion sources found in this pass do not evaluate SAM 2 or Cutie on hands. They do document the same setting the lab has. Bhalgat et al., ACCV 2024, on EPIC-Fields: rapid camera motion, frequent occlusions, limited visibility. Their 3D-centroid tracker beats the second-best 2D method by 7 Association Accuracy points and 4.5 IDF1, and cuts ID switches by 73% to 80% depending on category. They also use the tracks for amodal (occlusion-completed) segmentation. — [Bhalgat et al., ACCV 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2024/html/Bhalgat_3D-Aware_Instance_Segmentation_and_Tracking_in_Egocentric_Videos_ACCV_2024_paper.html)
- CERES (NeurIPS 2025) is egocentric referring VOS: segment the object named by a language query in first-person video. The authors say existing methods learn spurious correlations and fail under “rapid motion and frequent occlusions.” CERES adds depth as a geometric cue. No numeric occlusion-switch rate was in the abstract fetched here, and the task is language referring, not mask propagation from a confirmed frame. — [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/ec7195f76444e0b08c45b39b30b60f83-Abstract-Conference.html)
- EgoHOS (ECCV 2022, older) is per-image hand and interacted-object segmentation, 11,243 egocentric images, with contact boundaries. It is not a temporal propagator. The authors’ “seeing through the hand” application segments the hand and inpaints it with flow-guided video inpainting so the occluded object can be seen. That is evidence that hands are treated as an occluder to remove, not as a cue that the object identity changed. — [Zhang et al., ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136890125.pdf)
- ORMNet (2024) is also per-image egocentric hand-object segmentation. It uses hand features to disambiguate the interacted object and says prior work confuses interacting-object categories. No video propagation numbers were in the abstract. — [Su et al., arXiv:2407.05576](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05576v2)

### Inferences

- Similar appearance is a memory-matching failure, not a box-threshold failure. Cutie’s evidence is the DAVIS-to-MOSE drop and the gain from object-level queries. SAM 2’s evidence is the juggling-ball / horse examples plus SAM2Long’s measured 1.3–5.3 point recovery from not committing the uncertain mask. Neither paper reports a rate such as “32% of errors are neighbor switches.”
- A hand is, in this literature, an occluder and a distractor. SAM 2 can declare the object absent, or it can lock onto whatever visible region best matches memory, including the hand or a nearby part. EgoHOS treats the hand as something to segment and remove. No source here counts how often a propagator’s mask sits on the hand.
- Camera motion is named by Meta as a cause of lost tracks under viewpoint change, and by SAM 2 as a reason video frames are degraded (blur, resolution). The tracking papers in the next section are the ones that measure camera motion as a broken overlap test. VOS papers mostly absorb camera motion into appearance memory, which is why a nearby similar part can win the match when the camera moves.

### Gaps

- No fetched paper gives a numeric rate of masks placed on a neighboring part, on a hand, or on a same-color object for SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, or DEVA.
- No 2023–2026 VOS paper fetched here isolates head-mounted camera motion as an ablation (for example, fixed camera vs head-mounted on the same objects).
- CERES and ORMNet were not fetched beyond abstracts, so their tables are unused. Bhalgat’s 7 / 4.5 / 73–80% figures are versus “the second-best,” and the second-best method’s name was not in the abstract.
- SAM2Long, the SAM 2 paper, and the GitHub checkpoint card disagree on absolute \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\). Use SAM2Long only for its reported deltas.

## How do ByteTrack, BoT-SORT, OC-SORT, and open-vocabulary trackers decide to drop a track instead of attaching it to the wrong detection?

### Takeaway

They predict where the existing track should be, then refuse the match when overlap, direction, or appearance falls below a gate. The unmatched track is held as lost and is not emitted. A low-confidence or unmatched detection cannot repair that track by becoming a new box for the same id, except through ByteTrack’s second stage, which still requires IoU and still never births a track from a low-score box.

### Cited Findings

- ByteTrack (2021, older) keeps a Kalman prediction of each track. First association matches high-score detections to all tracks, including lost ones, by IoU or by a Re-ID distance, solved with the Hungarian algorithm. Second association matches leftover tracks only to low-score detections, and only by IoU, because occluded or blurred boxes have unreliable appearance. Unmatched low-score detections are deleted as background. Unmatched tracks go to a lost set. A lost track is deleted after it has been lost for more than 30 frames. New tracks are born only from unmatched high-score detections. Lost tracks are not part of the frame output. Default high-score threshold \(\tau=0.6\). In the linear assignment, IoU below 0.2 rejects the pair. On MOT17, with IoU only, ByteTrack reports 80.3 MOTA, 77.3 IDF1, 63.1 HOTA at 30 FPS on a V100. — [Zhang et al., ByteTrack, arXiv:2110.06864](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06864v3)
- ByteTrack’s own low-frame-rate result is the important negative. On BDD100K, which they describe as large camera motion and low frame rate, IoU as the first similarity is weak and Re-ID is stronger: first association by Re-ID and second by IoU reaches 45.5 mMOTA, 54.8 mIDF1, 9140 ID switches, versus IoU/IoU at 39.4 mMOTA, 48.9 mIDF1, 27902 ID switches (their Table 1). They state the general rule: location and motion are reliable for short-range matching; appearance is for long-range re-identification after a long occlusion. Using Re-ID on the low-score second association hurts, by about 1.0 MOTA on MOT17 relative to IoU, because those boxes are occluded or blurred. Applying the same two-stage idea to CenterTrack on half of MOT17 val moved MOTA 66.1 to 67.4, IDF1 64.2 to 74.0, and ID switches 528 to 144. — [Zhang et al., ByteTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06864v3)
- BoT-SORT (2022, older) uses the same rejection and the same lost buffer: similarity below 0.2 is rejected; lost tracks are kept 30 frames; detection threshold 0.6. It adds camera-motion compensation. IoU association assumes the Kalman box still overlaps the detection. Camera motion breaks that overlap and produces ID switches or false negatives. They estimate a global affine transform by image registration and correct the Kalman mean and covariance before association. MOT17-13 is their example: the camera is on a vehicle turning, and uncompensated predictions miss the detections. They also say static-camera sequences can be hurt by vibration or wind-driven drift (MOT20). — [Aharon et al., BoT-SORT, arXiv:2206.14651](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14651v2)
- BoT-SORT’s appearance gate is separate from the IoU gate. Appearance is an exponential moving average with momentum \(\alpha=0.9\), updated only from high-confidence detections, because crowds, occlusion, and blur corrupt the feature. A candidate is rejected before fusion if IoU distance exceeds \(\theta_{iou}=0.5\) or cosine distance exceeds \(\theta_{emb}=0.25\). The cost that remains is the minimum of the IoU distance and the cosine distance, not a weighted sum. On their MOT17 validation ablation, IoU alone is 78.4 MOTA, 81.5 IDF1, 69.1 HOTA; IoU-masked cosine is 78.3 / 81.0 / 68.7; the min-fusion is 78.5 / 82.1 / 69.2. So appearance barely moves detection metrics and slightly helps identity, and IoU alone is the low-compute choice they endorse. Reported MOT17 test for the full method: 80.5 MOTA, 80.2 IDF1, 65.0 HOTA. Linear interpolation is a post-process with a maximum gap of 20 frames, not the association decision. The experiments ran on an RTX 3060. — [Aharon et al., BoT-SORT](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14651v2)
- OC-SORT (CVPR 2023, older) argues that the drop decision is often a broken motion model rather than a missing detection. During occlusion the Kalman filter keeps predicting with no measurement, and the error grows with time. Their derivation: if position noise per frame is on the order of inter-frame displacement, a 10-frame occlusion can move the predicted center by about the object size. Their pedestrian example is a person of about \(50\times 300\) pixels and a 1-pixel variance. The linear model is, in their words, acceptable only for very short occlusions. — [Cao et al., OC-SORT, arXiv:2203.14360](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.14360v2)
- OC-SORT’s association cost is negative IoU plus \(\lambda=0.2\) times a direction penalty. Direction is computed from observations \(\Delta t=3\) apart, not from the Kalman velocity. The IoU gate is 0.3. Detection confidence is 0.6 except 0.4 on MOT20. A second stage, observation-centric recovery, matches unmatched detections to the last real observation of unmatched tracks rather than to the drifted prediction, and then rebuilds a virtual trajectory across the gap with constant velocity. Tracks still age out; the reference implementation uses `max_age=30`. On DanceTrack test, where appearance is similar, motion is non-linear, and occlusion and crossovers are frequent, and where ByteTrack and OC-SORT share YOLOX detections: ByteTrack 47.3 HOTA, 71.6 DetA, 31.4 AssA, 89.5 MOTA, 52.5 IDF1; OC-SORT 54.6 / 80.4 / 40.2 / 89.6 / 54.6. Adding linear interpolation afterward is a separate row: 55.1 HOTA, 40.4 AssA, 92.2 MOTA, 54.9 IDF1. On MOT17 test with shared private detections: ByteTrack 63.1 HOTA, 80.3 MOTA, 77.3 IDF1, 2196 ID switches; OC-SORT 63.2 HOTA, 78.0 MOTA, 77.5 IDF1, 1950 ID switches. KITTI, which they flag as a low frame rate of 10 FPS, is in the paper but the car/pedestrian cells were not cleanly readable in the text extraction used here. — [Cao et al., OC-SORT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.14360v2)
- OVTrack (CVPR 2023, older) is open-vocabulary multiple-object tracking: classes of interest can be named at test time and need not be the training set. Localization is class-agnostic; a text-embedding head assigns the class; association uses appearance only, because they say camera motion and heterogeneous motion are hard to model without video labels. At inference, a detection joins the track with maximum similarity if similarity exceeds \(\beta\), using both bidirectional softmax and cosine similarity. If it matches nothing, it starts a track only when its confidence exceeds \(\gamma\); otherwise it is discarded. The public config uses `match_score_thr=0.5`, `memo_frames=10`, `match_metric='bisoftmax'`, `match_with_cosine=True`. The paper’s symbols \(\beta\) and \(\gamma\) are not given numeric values in the excerpt; the config’s 0.5 is the concrete threshold in code, and its init thresholds are 0.0001, so the code and the prose should not be collapsed into one number. — [Li et al., OVTrack, CVPR 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf); [ovtrack_r50.py](https://github.com/SysCV/ovtrack/blob/main/configs/ovtrack-teta/ovtrack_r50.py)
- OVTrack is trained on static images, not on video, using CLIP-style distillation and diffusion-generated appearance pairs. On TAO validation, novel classes, the LVIS-only OVTrack row is TETA 27.8, LocA 48.8, AssocA 33.6, ClsA 1.5. The same row for base classes is TETA 35.5, LocA 49.3, AssocA 36.9, ClsA 20.2. DeepSORT with ViLD on novel classes is AssocA 14.7 and ClsA 2.3. Adding RegionCLIP raises OVTrack novel ClsA from 1.5 to 11.4 and novel TETA from 27.8 to 32.0, with AssocA still 33.2. Classification of rare classes remains the weak term; association is better than the DeepSORT baseline and still far from solved. Test-set novel ClsA for LVIS-only OVTrack is 1.8. — [Li et al., OVTrack, Table 1](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- TETA, the metric OVTrack uses, is the published separation of “where it is” from “whether the id is right” from “what class name it has.” LocA matches boxes to ground truth with no class check. AssocA scores identity only on well-localized matches. ClsA scores the class name only on well-localized matches. A wrong class does not by itself count as a wrong track, and a missed box does not have to be repaired by a class guess. The metric also evaluates inside local clusters so missing annotations in large-vocabulary sets do not automatically punish every extra box. — [Li et al., OVTrack, citing Li et al., TETA, ECCV 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf); [siyuanliii/TETA](https://github.com/siyuanliii/TETA/)
- OVTrack’s task still requires the names of the classes of interest at test time. The authors distinguish this from open-world tracking, where the class set is unknown and evaluation becomes recall-based. Novel classes are the LVIS rare classes, used as a proxy, not as a proof that unnamed parts can be tracked. — [Li et al., OVTrack](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)

### Inferences

- The drop rule is a failed gate, followed by silence. ByteTrack will not output the Kalman box as if it were a detection, and it will not let a low-score box start a new identity. That is the opposite of repairing a miss by asking for a new box and then displaying it.
- At about 1 Hz, or under head motion, the IoU gate is the part these papers say fails. ByteTrack’s BDD100K ablation is the direct evidence: low frame rate plus camera motion makes motion matching worse than appearance matching, and the failure looks like ID switches (27902 vs 9140 in that table). OC-SORT’s 10-frame error analysis is at video rate; a 1-second hole at 30 fps is already three times that 10-frame example, so a 1 Hz tracker that interpolates motion across one missed sample is outside the regime they call safe.
- DanceTrack is the similar-appearance stress test. Shared detections, ByteTrack association accuracy 31.4 versus OC-SORT 40.2. Localization is easy there (DetA 71.6–80.4) while identity is the failure. That matches a lab case of similar-looking parts: a detector can put a box on a part every frame and the tracker can still attach it to the wrong track if the gate is only overlap.
- Open-vocabulary MOT does not remove the label problem for unnamed parts. OVTrack’s novel-class classification accuracy is 1.5–11.4. Association is somewhat stronger than classification, and it is still an appearance match against a memory of 10 frames in the public config. If two parts look alike, appearance association is the same failure Cutie describes, without a mask propagator.

### Gaps

- OW-VISFormer was not read in this pass. Do not import claims about it.
- KITTI numeric rows for OC-SORT were not reliably extracted. The 10 FPS remark is cited; the scores are not.
- BoT-SORT’s \(\theta_{emb}=0.25\) was chosen from a histogram of a FastReID model trained on MOT17 pedestrians. It is not a general threshold for parts.
- No source here measures ByteTrack, BoT-SORT, or OC-SORT on a 1 Hz parts video.

## Which of these can run locally on one GPU or on CPU, without a closed label set, and what do they cost relative to a vision-language call on every sampled frame?

### Takeaway

SAM 2, XMem, Cutie, and DEVA’s propagator are class-agnostic and published with local code and weights. On one data-center GPU they run at interactive to real-time rates once a mask exists, which is a different cost structure from calling a vision-language model on every sampled frame. A sourced CPU frames-per-second number was not found. ByteTrack-style association is not itself a label set, but the detectors it was scored with are closed-set people detectors.

### Cited Findings

- SAM 2 has no category label set. Prompts define the segment. The SA-V collection “do[es] not impose semantic constraints,” and includes parts and subparts. Checkpoints and Apache 2.0 training code are released. The video predictor example runs under CUDA with bfloat16 autocast. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2); [facebookresearch/sam2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2/)
- Speed, paper measurement: one A100, batch size 1, PyTorch 2.3.1, CUDA 12.1, bfloat16, image encoder compiled. Hiera-B+ 43.8 FPS, Hiera-L 30.2 FPS. Image-only SAM 2 in the same paper’s Table 5 is 130.1 FPS versus SAM at 21.7 FPS, and the text says SAM 2 is 6× faster than SAM on the image task. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- Speed, GitHub measurement, not the same number: one A100, torch 2.5.1, CUDA 12.4, all components compiled. SAM 2.1 tiny/small/base+/large: 91.2 / 84.8 / 64.1 / 39.5 FPS at 38.9M / 46M / 80.8M / 224.4M parameters. The README says compiling only the image encoder is slower than compiling everything. — [facebookresearch/sam2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2/)
- A 2025 A40 measurement on 1080p LVOS v2 videos, PyTorch 2.5.1, CUDA 12.4: SAM 2.1 Large uses 5499 MB and 13.13 FPS; Base+ 4449 MB and 20.11 FPS; Small 4193 MB and 26.65 FPS; Tiny 4165 MB and 30.21 FPS. SAM2Long-Large on the same setup uses 6913 MB and 8.51 FPS. These figures are lower FPS than the GitHub A100 table. The hardware and the stated 1080p inputs differ, so this is a second measurement, not a correction of the A100 table. — [SAM2Plus, Sensors / PMC12252479](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12252479/)
- XMem (2022) is class-agnostic given a mask. Official speeds without automatic mixed precision: DAVIS 2016 29.6 FPS (\(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) 91.5), DAVIS 2017 val 22.6 FPS (86.2), YouTube-VOS 2018 22.6 FPS (\(\mathcal{G}\) 85.7). With mixed precision the same three are 40.3, 33.9, and 31.7 FPS. The authors say the mixed-precision numerical difference rarely changes the reported tenth. The repository’s plain-language claim is “expect ~20 FPS even with long videos (hardware dependent).” Supplement on a 32 GB V100, Long-time Video (3×): long-term memory cap 10,000 stores about 3,900 frames after consolidation at ratio \(r=10\), uses 1515 MB, and runs at 23.4 FPS; cap 500 uses 1168 MB at 35.3 FPS with \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) 87.2 versus 90.0 at the larger cap. — [hkchengrex/XMem RESULTS.md](https://github.com/hkchengrex/XMem/blob/main/docs/RESULTS.md); [XMem supplement](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136880633-supp.pdf)
- Cutie does not publish a single FPS in the sections read here. It says the MOSE gain over XMem comes “with a similar running time,” and that Cutie is three times faster than DeAOT while 4.2 \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) higher on MOSE. A concurrent single-transformer VOS line is cited as under 4 FPS. On BURST, Cutie-base with long-term memory uses 2.36 GB and scores HOTA 58.4 (val all) / 62.6 (test all); Cutie-small FIFO uses 1.35 GB. Inputs are resized so the short side is at most 480 px. The model is a ResNet pixel encoder plus a small object transformer, not a closed label set. — [Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)
- DEVA’s propagator is class-agnostic and is trained once. The image model is pluggable and is not called every frame: default merge every 5 frames, clip of 3 in semi-online mode. On BURST, EntitySeg as the image model needs no class labels and scores higher on uncommon classes than a COCO-class Mask2Former (val uncommon OWTA 50.5 vs 41.5). Grounding DINO is the text-prompt front-end when a name exists. The repository states no finetuning is required to insert another image model. — [Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf); [DEVA repository](https://github.com/hkchengrex/Tracking-Anything-with-DEVA)
- ByteTrack’s 30 FPS on a V100 is the full MOT17 system including the YOLOX detector, not the association step alone, and that detector is a closed person detector in the benchmark. BoT-SORT’s Re-ID extractor is a FastReID ResNeSt-50 trained on MOT17/MOT20 pedestrians; the tracker without Re-ID is the IoU-only ablation, which they say is the low-resource choice. Association itself (Kalman, Hungarian, IoU) does not contain a label set. — [Zhang et al., ByteTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06864v3); [Aharon et al., BoT-SORT](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14651v2)
- OVTrack’s backbone in the reported experiments is ResNet-50. It can name novel classes only through a vision-language head, and novel ClsA stays low (1.5 without RegionCLIP, 11.4 with it, TAO val). It is not a no-label-set segmenter. — [Li et al., OVTrack](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)

### Inferences

- The no-API-key path is a local propagator: SAM 2, XMem, Cutie, or DEVA with a local class-agnostic image front-end (SAM point grid or EntitySeg). A vision-language model remains optional and belongs at the sparse confirmation step, which is the role DEVA already gives the image model (every 5 frames in their default, or only when a human or a model confirms a new object).
- Relative cost, using only the speeds above: on one A100, SAM 2.1 Large processes on the order of 40 frames per second of GPU time, and Hiera-B+ in the paper is 43.8 FPS. A 1 Hz sampled stream is roughly one frame of that work per second of video, before counting multiple objects. SAM 2 decodes objects separately, so object count multiplies decoder and memory work, not the image encoding. XMem at about 20 FPS on older hardware is the same shape of result: one GPU frame is much cheaper than one remote vision-language request, but this note has no priced API latency to divide by.
- The 1080p A40 numbers (about 13 FPS for SAM 2.1 Large, about 5.5 GB) are the more conservative local-GPU figure if the lab’s frames are 1080p and the GPU is not an A100. A single consumer GPU in the 8–12 GB class is in range of the A40 memory figures for the smaller SAM 2.1 variants (about 4.2–4.5 GB for tiny through base+ at 1080p) and of XMem’s 1.2–2.4 GB long-video figures. Those are memory footprints, not a guarantee of real-time playback.
- Closed-set MOT (ByteTrack, BoT-SORT, OC-SORT as benchmarked) does not solve unnamed parts. Reusing only their gate-and-drop logic on top of an open proposer is a different system from the published MOT17 numbers.

### Gaps

- No official CPU frames-per-second figure was found for SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, or DEVA. “Hardware dependent ~20 FPS” for XMem is not a CPU measurement.
- No source here prices a vision-language locate call or reports its latency on this lab’s providers. A numerical “N times cheaper” claim would be invented.
- Q4KM.ai’s 12 GB / 24 GB SAM 2.1 guidance was not used; it is a secondary hardware page, not a paper.
- DEVA Table 5’s FPS column was not reliably extracted, so DEVA’s own frames-per-second is uncited.

## What design separates “the region moved” from “this is still that object,” so a miss is not repaired by inventing a new box?

### Takeaway

The published separation is: a motion or memory module carries the confirmed object, and a second test decides whether a new observation is the same object. If that test fails, the system emits a miss or keeps the carried mask, and it does not write a new box onto the old identity. Inventing a box is what happens when a second localizer is asked to fill the miss.

### Cited Findings

- SAM 2 separates presence from shape. The occlusion head can say the object is not visible. Invisible frames get an occlusion embedding instead of being treated as ordinary appearance memory. The carried state is the memory bank built from a confirmed prompt, not a new proposal. The authors’ recovery tool for a lost or confused object is an additional prompt on a later frame. — [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- SAM2Long’s critique is that SAM 2 does not fully use that separation. The occlusion score and the three masks exist, but default inference still commits the single highest-IoU mask into memory, including on frames where \(|o_t|\) is low. Their design keeps multiple hypotheses until the video-level score prefers one, and it refuses to store low-confidence or occluded frames as appearance memory. That is a delay of the identity decision, not a new detector. — [Ding et al., SAM2Long](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16268v3)
- XMem separates timescales. Sensory memory is the short-term “it moved smoothly” state and is allowed to drift. Working memory and the protected first-frame memory are the identity reference. Long-term prototypes exist so a reappearing object can match an old observation rather than only the last frame. The readout is a soft assignment over memory locations; it is not a new detection. — [Cheng and Schwing, XMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07115)
- Cutie separates pixel correspondence from object identity. Pixel memory answers “which pixels moved.” Object queries and foreground-background masked attention answer “which object that is,” so a distractor pixel match does not become the foreground. The gain they measure for that split is 8.7 \(\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}\) on MOSE against XMem in the without-MOSE training comparison (64.0 vs 56.3). — [Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)
- DEVA is the explicit two-module design. `Prop` carries the region. The image segmenter is not asked to redraw the box in order to keep the identity. A new image mask is the same object only if IoU with the propagated mask exceeds 0.5; otherwise it passes through as its own segment. A propagated object survives detector misses until five consecutive failed associations, then it is deleted rather than matched to a distant proposal. The authors say classical tracking-by-detection treats image detections as immutable and therefore “depends heavily on the quality of per-image detections.” Their in-clip consensus exists to reject lone image proposals as noise. — [Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- ByteTrack separates prediction from emission. The Kalman box is where the region is expected to be. A detection is the same track only if the assignment survives the 0.2 IoU gate (and, if used, the first-stage appearance term). If nothing matches, the track is lost and is not drawn. Low-score detections may reattach only to an existing unmatched track, and only by IoU, and they are thrown away if they do not. They cannot create the identity. — [Zhang et al., ByteTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06864v3)
- BoT-SORT separates camera motion from object motion. The global transform explains how the whole image moved; the Kalman state after that correction is the object’s motion. Appearance is a third signal, gated at cosine distance 0.25 and IoU distance 0.5, and it is not updated from low-confidence, occluded, or blurred crops. Interpolation of gaps up to 20 frames is applied after tracking, as compensation for missing detections, and is not an association. — [Aharon et al., BoT-SORT](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14651v2)
- OC-SORT separates the filter’s guess from the last real observation. After a gap, re-association uses the last detection, not the drifted prediction, and direction consistency uses observations three frames apart. The virtual fill-in between the two real observations is linear and is used to repair the filter, not to claim a new detection. Their DanceTrack interpolation row is reported separately from the tracker. — [Cao et al., OC-SORT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.14360v2)
- TETA separates the three errors the lab is currently collapsing. A frame can be a localization miss (LocA), an identity swap on a good box (AssocA), or a wrong name on a good box (ClsA). OVTrack’s novel-class numbers show a localization score (LocA 48.8) with almost no classification (ClsA 1.5). The metric’s point is that those are not the same failure and should not be fixed by the same mechanism. — [Li et al., OVTrack](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- The Sep 2025 MOSEv2 motion module is a narrower version of the same split: a kinematic state predicts where the object went during occlusion, and that prediction is a spatial prior added to segmentation logits. It does not re-detect the object. The authors introduce it because SAM 2’s memory does not by itself keep identity across long occlusions and similar instances. — [arXiv:2509.15781](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15781v1)
- Bhalgat et al. make the same split in egocentric video with geometry: a 3D centroid track is the identity, and the 2D mask is the region. On EPIC-Fields that reduced ID switches by 73% to 80% versus the second-best 2D method. — [Bhalgat et al., ACCV 2024](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ACCV2024/html/Bhalgat_3D-Aware_Instance_Segmentation_and_Tracking_in_Egocentric_Videos_ACCV_2024_paper.html)

### Inferences

- The lab’s current second vision-language locate call is a new proposal, not a propagation. Under DEVA’s rules it would be merged into the existing object only when it overlaps the carried mask, and discarded or given a new id otherwise. Under ByteTrack’s rules it would be ignored unless it overlapped the predicted track, and it would never be required in order for the track to exist.
- A miss and a wrong region are different outputs of the same test. If the carried mask is withheld whenever the occlusion score is uncertain, the frame is a miss and the neighbor is not labeled. If the highest-overlap mask is written anyway, the frame is a wrong-region detection and later frames inherit it. SAM2Long is the paper that measures the second choice as an error-accumulation problem.
- Short-gap linear interpolation, which the lab already refuses to call a detection, is also how BoT-SORT and OC-SORT treat interpolation: a post-process or a filter repair, reported on its own row, not a new observation.
- For unnamed, similar, partly hand-covered parts, the design that matches the evidence is class-agnostic mask memory from confirmed frames (SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, or DEVA’s propagator), plus an explicit non-match outcome (occlusion score, failed IoU merge, or lost track) that leaves the frame empty. A new box is allowed when a later confirmed prompt or a non-overlapping image proposal says a new object appeared. Appearance embeddings and optical flow can feed the match test; the papers above do not treat either one as the identity by itself. Cutie’s result says pixel appearance alone is the distractor failure. ByteTrack’s result says flow-like motion alone fails when the frame gap or the camera motion is large.

### Gaps

- No paper fetched here states a design for the lab’s exact constraint set: 1 Hz samples, pluggable vision-language providers, local MSER and contrast contours, and a requirement that uncertain geometric checks neither hide a visible part nor paint the wrong region.
- Whether a carried mask should be shown during a hand occlusion (amodal completion, as Bhalgat discusses) or withheld (SAM 2 occlusion head) is not settled by a head-to-head number on parts.
- The 0.5 IoU merge in DEVA and the 0.2 / 0.3 IoU gates in the trackers are published operating points, not transferable thresholds for this lab’s parts. They are cited only as the mechanism those systems use to refuse a match.
