# VLM localization: wrong boxes, and who should emit them

Scope note. Today is 27 October 2026. Years below are publication or preprint years. Results from 2022–early 2024 are marked older. This note does not treat a prompt change or a stricter JSON schema as a fix. The question is which component is allowed to emit the box.

## What do RefCOCO-family and later localization benchmarks report for vision-language models versus specialist detectors, especially on small objects, occlusion, and similar neighbors?

### Takeaway
RefCOCO-family accuracy above 90% is a saturated, noisy, mostly large-object number and does not describe small, similar, or partly hidden parts. On benchmarks built for those cases, a general vision-language model that emits the box falls to the low tens of percent or below, while a detector that regresses boxes and uses language only to choose among them stays far higher. The same model can score well on RefCOCO and still be near zero on objects that occupy well under 1% of the frame.

### Cited Findings
- RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg are referring-expression comprehension (REC) benchmarks on MS-COCO images. A prediction is counted correct at Acc@0.5 when the predicted box has intersection-over-union (IoU) above 0.5 with the ground-truth box. Mean expression length is about 3.6 words on RefCOCO and RefCOCO+, and 8.4 words on RefCOCOg, with fewer than 80 categories. — [Chen et al., Ref-L4, arXiv:2406.16866 / CVPR Workshops 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16866)
- A manual audit of the validation and test sets found labeling error rates of 14% on RefCOCO, 24% on RefCOCO+, and 5% on RefCOCOg (typos, expression–instance mismatches, and bad boxes). After those instances were removed, four large multimodal models gained 1.57 to 3.08 accuracy points. CogVLM-Grounding is reported at 92.44% on the original RefCOCO benchmark and 94.58% on the cleaned version. The authors treat scores above 90% as saturation, not as proof of fine localization. — [Chen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16866)
- Older specialist numbers, as compiled in 2024, already sat in the same band as grounding-tuned language models. On RefCOCO val / testA / testB Acc@0.5: MDETR 86.75 / 89.58 / 81.41; Grounding DINO 90.56 / 93.19 / 88.24; UNINEXT-L 91.43 / 93.73 / 88.93. Generalists that emit coordinates: Shikra 87.01 / 90.61 / 80.24; Ferret 87.49 / 91.35 / 82.45; Qwen-VL 89.36 / 92.26 / 85.34; Groma (language model points at a proposed region, it does not regress the box) 89.53 / 92.09 / 86.26. RefCOCO+ testB, the split that forbids location words, is lower for everyone: Grounding DINO 75.92, Shikra 72.12, Ferret 73.14, Qwen-VL 77.21, Groma 78.05, MDETR 70.62. — [Ma et al., Groma, arXiv:2404.13013, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13013). These Grounding DINO REC numbers conflict with a 2025 table that lists an REC-trained Grounding DINO much lower (see Gaps).
- Ref-L4 (2025) has 45,341 expressions, 365 categories, mean length 24.2 words, and 22,813 unique words. Small means \(\sqrt{hw} < 128\) (9,345 expressions), medium 128–256, large above 256. On the combined set, Acc@0.5 / Acc@0.75 / Acc@0.9: GPT-4V 9.91 / 1.19 / 0.12; GroundingGPT 60.84 / 40.48 / 12.00; Qwen-VL-Chat 73.80 / 58.05 / 37.16; CogVLM-Grounding 81.70 / 70.77 / 48.35. By size, Acc@0.5 small / medium / large: GPT-4V 2.13 / 10.29 / 14.93; Shikra-7B 43.91 / 75.98 / 60.60; Qwen-VL-Chat 47.66 / 79.80 / 82.01; CogVLM-Grounding 75.06 / 86.43 / 77.91; GroundingGPT 24.43 / 67.67 / 75.09. The best box-emitting model still loses about 11 points from medium to small, and its Acc@0.9 is 48.35, so a “correct” RefCOCO hit is often a loose box. — [Chen et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16866); CogVLM-Grounding small Acc@0.5 75.06 also in the released evaluation log — [JierunChen/Ref-L4](https://github.com/jierunchen/ref-l4)
- A 2026 Nature table compares models that emit boxes with a pipeline that lets detectors emit boxes and GPT-4o select. Ref-L4 val Acc@0.5: DeepSeek-VL2-Tiny 3.20, Qwen2-VL-2B 18.55, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite 46.01, MQADet + Grounding DINO 59.35, MQADet + YOLO-World 57.86, MQADet + OmDet-Turbo 51.66. On the easier RefCOCO testA Acc@0.5 the ranking flips for the small grounding VLM: Qwen2-VL-2B 76.99 versus Gemini-2.0-Flash-Lite 59.87 and MQADet + Grounding DINO 60.03. The detector-plus-language pipeline wins on the harder benchmark, not on saturated RefCOCO. — [Nature Scientific Reports table, s41598-026-36936-x](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-36936-x/tables/4)
- GroundingME (Li et al., CVPR 2026; 1,005 examples) was built because models were already near or above 90% on RefCOCO and nearly 90% on Ref-L4. It scores Acc@0.5 under greedy decoding. Images are much larger than RefCOCO (square-root area 1,500–7,680 versus 83–610). Instance-area-ratio quartiles are (0.16%, 1.0%, 2.7%). Intra-class instance-count quartiles are (5, 7, 12), so several lookalikes are the typical case, not a corner case. Samples with a class count below 3, or a box covering more than half the image, were removed. — [Li et al., arXiv:2512.17495](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1)
- On GroundingME, no-thinking, the best listed model is Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B at 45.1% Acc@0.5. Its breakdown: Discriminative average 69.6 (Appearance 71.2, Component 74.0, Text 84.0, State 50.0); Spatial average 49.7 (Relationship 62.7, Counting 36.7); Limited average 54.0 (Occlusion 72.7, Small 35.3); Rejection 0.0 on every subcategory. Most of 25 models score 0% on Rejection. Qwen3-VL dense scaling from 2B to 32B moves overall accuracy from 21.1% to 39.5% and does not fix rejection. General models collapse on the small subset: Llama-4-Scout Small 0.0 and Occlusion 7.3 (overall 8.9); Llama-4-Maverick Small 0.7 and Occlusion 9.3. Thinking mode raises overall accuracy (gains of 4.7 to 7.4 points on the models they tested) and can produce a little rejection, but the paper says thinking hurts perception-heavy subtasks relative to reasoning-heavy ones. The project leaderboard’s thinking run of Qwen3-VL-A22B is 49.8 overall, with Limited 45.0 and Rejection 5.5. — [Li et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1); [leaderboard](https://groundingme.github.io/)
- SOREC (Goto et al., ICCV 2025) is 100,000 referring expressions for objects in driving scenes. Every target box occupies less than 1% of the image; a typical box is about 0.05% of the image. Expressions average 25.5 words, about seven times RefCOCO’s 3.52. Zero-shot MM-GroundingDINO (Swin-T, pretrained on Objects365, GoldG, GRIT, and V3Det) has Acc@0.5 of 0.6 on val, 1.0 on test-A, and 0.2 on test-B. The same line of model, in this paper’s RefCOCO table, is 50.4 / 57.2 / 43.2 zero-shot and 89.2 / 91.9 / 86.0 after full fine-tuning. On SOREC, full fine-tuning of 173M parameters still only reaches Acc@0.5 of 63.7 (val), 69.6 (test-A), and 55.6 (test-B) when trained on the large split. The authors state that recent models exceed 90% on RefCOCO test sets and that small-object REC was still unsolved because the datasets did not contain these boxes. — [Goto et al., arXiv:2510.03701](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03701)
- When the same class of model is scored as a detector rather than as an REC system, the gap to a specialist remains large. Prompted to emit a labeled box for every COCO category, Qwen3-VL-8B’s raw outputs score 20.43 AP on COCO val2017. A supervised DETR-R50, an older specialist, scores 42.0 AP. Of 66,590 Qwen3-VL boxes, 68.1% match no ground-truth object of that class at IoU 0.5. Across the paper’s four localization benchmarks, 58% to 68% of emitted regions are not valid objects or events. — [Propose and Attend / MTLA, arXiv:2607.05978, 2026](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2607.05978)
- Zero-shot open-set detectors, which regress boxes with a detection head, sit in a different range. Older Grounding DINO Swin-L is listed at 52.5 COCO zero-shot AP. Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro (2024, ViT-L, Grounding-20M) reaches 54.3 COCO zero-shot AP, 55.7 LVIS-minival AP, and 47.6 LVIS-val AP. DINO-X Pro (late 2024) reaches 56.0 COCO, 59.8 LVIS-minival, and 52.4 LVIS-val box AP, and 63.3 / 56.5 AP on LVIS rare classes. These are category-prompt detection numbers, not referring-expression numbers, and several training sets overlap the evaluation images in older rows (the DINO-X paper marks those in gray). — [Grounding DINO 1.5, arXiv:2405.10300](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10300v2); [DINO-X, arXiv:2411.14347](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14347v1)
- A 2025 zero-shot REC workflow that never asks the VLM to draw the box reports an “organic” GPT-4o baseline under 10% Acc@0.5 on RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg. The same GPT-4o, asked only true/false about boxes from a COCO-clean YOLO-World detector, reaches 81.2 / 76.3 / 75.9 Acc@0.5. Zero-shot Grounding DINO in that table is 50.4 / 57.2 / 43.2 on RefCOCO val / testA / testB. — [arXiv:2509.09958](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09958v2)

### Inferences
- A second locate call that returns a neighbor’s box is the behavior GroundingME measures under Discriminative and Rejection, and the behavior Ref-L4 measures when Acc@0.5 is high but Acc@0.9 is not. The model has often selected an instance, not failed to see the scene.
- For parts whose area is a fraction of a percent of a fixed or head-mounted frame, RefCOCO is the wrong reference point. SOREC’s near-zero zero-shot Acc@0.5, and GroundingME’s 35.3% small-object Acc@0.5 for the strongest model tested, are the relevant band.
- Occlusion is not the same failure as small size. On GroundingME the best no-thinking model is at 72.7% on occlusion and 35.3% on small. A partly visible but tiny part is closer to the small subset.
- Specialist detectors win on detection AP and on hard referring sets when they own the box. They do not win merely by being “multimodal.” Grounding DINO’s RefCOCO score and a chat model’s RefCOCO score can both exceed 90% while their small-object and open-world detection behavior diverges.

### Gaps
- Groma’s 2024 table lists Grounding DINO at 90.56 RefCOCO val Acc@0.5. The 2025 verification paper’s Table II lists REC-trained Grounding DINO at 77.3 and 72.5 on the RefCOCO test columns it fills in, with val left blank in the extract. Checkpoint, fine-tune, and split are not aligned in the sources read here. Both numbers are reported; they are not interchangeable.
- No RefCOCO split is defined as “similar neighbors” or “partial occlusion.” RefCOCO+ testB is harder because location words are forbidden, not because the objects are small.
- Florence-2’s official RefCOCO table was not retrieved in this pass. A course slide quoting Florence-2 at 93.4 / 88.3 / 91.2 was not used.
- GroundingME evaluates models that emit boxes. It does not publish a paired Grounding DINO or SAM number on the same 1,005 images.
- SOREC is driving imagery, not a tabletop. The size regime (under 1% of the image, often ~0.05%) is the transferable fact, not the object categories.

## Which papers document box hallucination, grounding on the wrong instance, or copying a nearby object’s extent, and what rates do they report?

### Takeaway
Three different failures are measured, and they are not the same rate. The model invents a box for something that is not there. The model puts the box on a similar neighbor. The model places a box near the right object but outside its extent. Papers that study caption hallucination do not measure these spatial rates. No paper read here reports a tabletop rate of “the predicted box is a copy of the neighbor’s box.”

### Cited Findings
- GroundingME Rejection (201 of 1,005 samples, 20.0%) asks the model to output no box when the description does not match. Under greedy decoding most of 25 models score 0% and instead emit a box. The paper’s case figure describes the wrong trajectory as boxing the distractor. Human check on 100 Discriminative/Rejection items (51 of them Rejection): 91% accuracy, 88.24% precision, 93.75% recall, 90.91% F1 for the reject/non-reject label. Data-mixture training that adds negative samples raises rejection accuracy from 0% to 27.9% for a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B; that is a training change, not evidence that a prompt fixes an off-the-shelf model. — [Li et al., CVPR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1); human numbers in the [supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Li_GroundingME_Exposing_the_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)
- Propose and Attend (2026) defines a localization hallucination as a proposed region with IoU below 0.5 against every ground-truth region of the same class. Qwen3-VL: 68.1% of 66,590 COCO detections are hallucinations by that rule. The model’s own token probabilities barely separate grounded boxes from hallucinated ones, because coordinate tokens become near-deterministic once generation has committed. Coordinates are emitted in a [0, 1000] grid, digit by digit. Attention of a grounded prediction concentrates inside the claimed box; attention of a hallucination (their examples include a cow labeled “horse” and a phantom dining table) spreads onto other context. Re-ranking with their attention score moves Qwen3-VL-8B from 20.43 AP to 32.12 AP at one sample and 37.01 AP at 16 samples, still short of DETR-R50 at 42.0 AP. — [arXiv:2607.05978](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2607.05978)
- GUI grounding papers split the same two errors, on interface elements rather than tabletop parts. Zhang et al., CVPR 2026, name Confusion Hallucination (a distractor element is selected because it looks or means something similar) and Fabricated Hallucination (a nonexistent element is given plausible coordinates). Their benchmark’s hallucination subset is constructed as about 40% confusion and 60% fabricated; that is the test-set mix, not a model error rate. Per-model localization accuracy and rejection rate were not extracted cleanly from Table 2 in this pass. — [Zhang et al., CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Zhang_Exposing_and_Evaluating_Hallucinations_for_GUI_Grounding_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)
- “Understanding GUI Agent Localization Biases through Logit Sharpness” (arXiv:2506.15425, 2025) is summarized as four outcomes: correct; biased hallucination (near the target but outside its box) 19.5%; misleading hallucination (coordinates land on a different element) 20.0%; confusion hallucination (coordinates land on nothing meaningful) 1.4%. The 19.5 / 20.0 / 1.4 figures were taken from the paper’s alphaxiv summary, not from a direct PDF reading. Biased hallucination is the near-miss extent. Misleading hallucination is the neighbor. — [alphaxiv summary of arXiv:2506.15425](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2506.15425)
- The verification-first REC paper argues that asking a VLM to choose among many boxes at once causes cross-box interference, and that a neighbor distractor is easier to reject when each box is judged alone. With the same proposals and the same VLM, true/false verification beats a single selection prompt by about 6 to 10 Acc@0.5 points on most splits, and by 18.0 points on average across three benchmarks and two VLMs. Their two-box calculation is a model of that interference, not a measured neighbor-copy rate. — [arXiv:2509.09958](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09958v2)
- Groma (2024) reports a concrete small-object failure of coordinate-emitting models on LVIS-Ground (4,299 images, 1,203 categories, 3.7 targets per image on average). Average recall: Shikra 4.9 (AR@s 0.1, AR@0.75 2.0); MiniGPT-v2 11.4 (AR@s 0.3); Ferret 16.8 (AR@s 1.6); Groma 28.8 (AR@s 8.7, AR@0.75 30.3). The authors say these models usually emit one box per image because REC training has one target per query, and that small-object recall is near zero because RefCOCO, RefCOCOg, RefCOCO+, and Flickr30k lack small boxes. — [Ma et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13013)
- ChatRex (Jiang et al., submitted 27 Nov 2024, revised 11 Mar 2025) states that Qwen2-VL, called a state-of-the-art multimodal model at the time, reaches only 43.9 recall on COCO, and treats that as a perception gap of models that predict coordinates with the language model. — [arXiv:2411.18363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18363)
- Dual-Agent (11 Oct 2026) measures what happens when a vision-language correction overwrites the tracker. On SecVOS, 17 committed corrections were applied while SAM 3 was already correct: SAM 3 IoU 0.961 versus the corrected IoU 0.026. Thirty-nine commits rescued a miss (0.000 to 0.856) and 32 rescued drift (0.035 to 0.903). Of 88 commits, 67 improved, 19 worsened, and 2 were neutral. The paper’s own reading is that the harm case is an unnecessary intervention overriding a correct track. That is a measured wrong-region overwrite, not a measured copy of the neighbor’s exact extent. — [Bundele et al., arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- Geigle, Timofte, and Glavaš (EMNLP 2024) study a different claim: adding referring-expression and grounded-captioning losses does not meaningfully reduce object hallucination in open captions, and forcing a box at inference time only slightly reduces it. Their qualitative note is that a generated box usually does not stop the caption from naming absent content. They do not report a rate of wrong boxes, neighbor selection, or copied extents. — [arXiv:2406.14492](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14492v1)
- An informal 50-image probe (not peer reviewed) asked models to box objects in crowded “Wimmel” pictures and to find a telescope that was not present. Mean GIoU was 0.13 for Gemini Pro, −0.60 for Claude, and −0.67 for GPT-4o. Telescope hallucination rates were 96% (GPT-4o), 30% (Claude), and 12% (Gemini), with Gemini’s false alarms tending toward cameras or binoculars. Sample size is 50 and there is no published protocol. It is consistent with the peer-reviewed rejection failure, and it is not a benchmark result. — [Khandpur, Wimmelbench](https://kabirk.com/wimmelbench.html)

### Inferences
- The lab’s “second locate call boxes a neighbor” matches Confusion / Misleading hallucination and GroundingME’s distractor box, not a missing detection. The box is a positive, confident region on the wrong instance.
- “Copying a nearby extent” is only partly documented. The GUI logit paper separates a near-miss (19.5%, outside the true box but close) from a different element (20.0%). Dual-Agent’s harmful commits are not near-misses: IoU falls from 0.961 to 0.026, which means the new box does not overlap the correct one. Keeping that box replaces a good region with a different one.
- Token probability is not a usable keep/drop score for coordinate output. The 2026 detection study says those probabilities conflate grounding with input ambiguity and then become nearly deterministic.
- A model can name an object and still attach a region that fails IoU 0.5. On COCO that was 68.1% of Qwen3-VL’s emitted boxes. Naming and localizing are not one competence.

### Gaps
- No source read here reports the fraction of natural-image errors whose box coordinates match a neighbor’s box exactly, for tabletop or egocentric parts.
- GUI-HalluBench Table 2 model-by-model localization accuracy and rejection rate were not extracted.
- The 19.5 / 20.0 / 1.4 GUI split was not re-read from the PDF.
- Wimmelbench is a blog post. It should not be cited as a model’s official hallucination rate.
- Adversarial box-permutation attacks exist (arXiv:2405.09981) but measure attack success, not the natural rate, so they are not used as a failure rate here.

## How do grounded detectors split the language query from box regression, and what still fails when two instances look alike?

### Takeaway
Grounding DINO, OWL-ViT, and their 2024–2025 successors never ask an autoregressive language model to write coordinates. A text encoder embeds the query, a detection decoder or a per-token box head regresses boxes, and the language link is a similarity score. Florence-2 is the exception in this family: it is a sequence model that emits quantized location tokens. Lookalike instances remain a ranking problem. The text embedding is shared by every instance of the described thing, so the highest score can be the neighbor. Later models add a visual prompt (a box or a point on the desired instance) exactly for the cases where words do not separate instances.

### Cited Findings
- Grounding DINO (Liu et al., preprint 2023, ECCV 2024) is a dual encoder and a single decoder. An image backbone and a text backbone are fused in a feature enhancer. Language-guided query selection takes the top 900 image tokens by maximum similarity to text tokens: \(\mathrm{Top}_{900}(\max(X_I X_T^\top))\). A cross-modality decoder refines those queries. Boxes are trained with L1 and GIoU, the standard DETR regression losses. Classification is a contrastive dot product between object queries and language tokens, not a generated coordinate string. For REC, the paper says to take the output object with the largest score. For detection, category names are concatenated into one text prompt. The public interface returns up to 900 boxes, each with a similarity to every input word, then thresholds boxes and text separately. — [arXiv:2303.05499](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.05499v5); [IDEA-Research/GroundingDINO](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/GroundingDINO/)
- MM-Grounding-DINO keeps that design, changes the contrastive classification head, and stops sharing decoder parameters across layers. The Hugging Face model card reports zero-shot gains to 50.6 AP on COCO (+2.2) and 31.9 on LVIS (the LVIS delta was truncated in the card text retrieved here). — [Hugging Face MM-Grounding-DINO](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/mm-grounding-dino)
- Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro (2024) “preserves the core architecture of Grounding DINO” with a ViT-L backbone, early fusion, and Grounding-20M images. Reported zero-shot AP: 54.3 COCO, 55.7 LVIS-minival, 47.6 LVIS-val. The Edge variant restricts cross-modal fusion to high-level features for speed (75.2 FPS under TensorRT in the paper’s setting; 36.2 AP zero-shot on LVIS-minival). — [arXiv:2405.10300](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10300v2)
- DINO-X (late 2024) uses the same encoder–decoder and the same box head: language-guided query selection, decoder updates, then “a simple MLP layer that predicts the corresponding bounding box coordinates,” with L1 and GIoU regression and contrastive alignment to the prompt. New inputs are a visual prompt (box or point, taken from T-Rex2) and a customized prompt. The paper says text covers everyday detection, while visual prompts cover cases “where text prompts fall short due to data scarcity and descriptive limitations.” Masks come from a separate mask head (Mask2Former / Mask DINO style dot product), not from the language model. Zero-shot box AP: 56.0 COCO, 59.8 LVIS-minival, 52.4 LVIS-val. — [arXiv:2411.14347](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14347v1)
- OWL-ViT (Minderer et al., ECCV 2022, older) removes CLIP’s pooled image token and attaches a light classification head and a box head to each image-transformer token. Open-vocabulary classification replaces the classifier weights with text embeddings from the text tower. The text tower is a contrastive text encoder, not a decoder that writes coordinates. Training uses bipartite matching. One or many text queries can be scored against the same set of boxes. — [Hugging Face OWL-ViT documentation](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/model_doc/owlvit). Grounding DINO 1.5’s fine-tune comparison table lists OWL-ST+FT (CLIP L/14) at 54.4 and 46.1 on the LVIS columns of that table. — [arXiv:2405.10300](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10300v2)
- Florence-2 (Microsoft, 2023–2024) does the opposite split. A secondary model description states that every task is sequence-to-sequence: DaViT image tokens plus a text prompt go through an encoder–decoder, which emits text and location tokens. Box coordinates are quantized vocabulary items for \((x_0,y_0,x_1,y_1)\). It is a specialist sequence model trained on a large grounding corpus (the README cites FLD-5B: 126 million images and 5.4 billion annotations; Florence-2-base 0.23B, Florence-2-large 0.77B), not a general chat model asked to invent a JSON box. Official RefCOCO numbers were not re-read from the paper in this pass. — [third-party architecture notes](https://github.com/anyantudre/Florence-2-Vision-Language-Model); paper of record is [arXiv:2311.06242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06242)
- Grounded SAM, as used in the Grounded-SAM-2 codebase, chains the two jobs. Florence-2 or a Grounding DINO-style detector proposes boxes from a task prompt such as referring-expression segmentation. SAM 2 then consumes the box as a prompt and predicts the mask. The mask decoder never receives the referring sentence. Video memory and the occlusion head live in SAM 2, not in the phrase encoder. — [IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2/); [image demo](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2/blob/main/grounded_sam2_florence2_image_demo.py)
- Groma (2024) states the design claim directly: localization “requires little semantic understanding but demands perceptual skills, which is typically out of the scope of an LLM’s expertise.” Its region proposer is a class-agnostic Deformable DETR head on DINOv2 features, producing up to 300 boxes. The Vicuna-7B language model then refers to proxy tokens `<r1>…<rn>` that are already anchored to those boxes. It reports RefCOCO val 89.53, competitive with Grounding DINO’s 90.56 in the same table, and the LVIS-Ground small-object gap above. — [arXiv:2404.13013](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13013)
- ChatRex (2024–2025) feeds boxes from a universal proposal network into the language model and trains the language model to output box indices. The regression task becomes retrieval. The stated motivation is Qwen2-VL’s 43.9 COCO recall when the language model predicts coordinates itself. — [arXiv:2411.18363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18363)
- What remains hard when two instances match the words: Grounding DINO’s REC rule is top-1 by score, so two high similarities produce one winner and no identity. GroundingME, whose boxes were themselves proposed by Grounding DINO and then filtered to scenes with many instances of the same class, still shows a State-discrimination Acc@0.5 of 50.0 and a Small Acc@0.5 of 35.3 for the best no-thinking vision-language model, and 0% rejection. DINO-X’s authors add a visual exemplar because text is the weak channel for long-tail and hard-to-describe instances. The verification paper’s measured gain from judging one box at a time (6 to 10 points, same proposals) is evidence that the failure is which box is chosen, after boxes already exist. — [Grounding DINO](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.05499v5); [GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1); [DINO-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14347v1); [arXiv:2509.09958](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09958v2)

### Inferences
- The architectural split the lab is asking about is already the standard in this detector line. Language embeds the query. A box head, trained with L1 and GIoU, emits the region. A later mask model may refine the extent from that box. The language model is not in the coordinate path.
- Florence-2 shows that a sequence model can emit good boxes when it is trained as a detector with location tokens and a huge grounding set. That does not license a general chat model, sampled in JSON, to be the per-frame box generator. ChatRex and Groma were written to get the language model off that job.
- Two similar parts on a table are the case visual prompts were added to solve. A shared text embedding cannot name which of two red parts is the tracked one. A reference crop is closer to T-Rex2 / DINO-X’s visual prompt than to another sentence. If that second call still returns the neighbor, the failure is instance ranking, and the literature’s response is to keep the detector’s boxes and change how selection works, not to ask the language model to redraw the extent.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of OWL-ViT versus a 2026 chat model on a same-instance-versus-neighbor split was found.
- Florence-2 RefCOCO figures were not taken from the paper.
- DINO-X does not publish a “two identical objects, text prompt only” error rate. The visual-prompt motivation is stated, not quantified as a confusion rate.
- Grounded SAM’s original 2024 assembly paper was not re-read; the pipeline above is from the Grounded-SAM-2 repository and demos.

## What does the literature say about using a vision-language model only to confirm or reject a track, and are there ablations of calling frequency versus accuracy?

### Takeaway
The strongest 2025–2026 result says the vision-language model should judge whether the tracker is still on the target, and should be called on a few percent of frames. Calling it every frame is described as expensive and harmful, because a wrong correction is written into tracker memory and then propagated. The box on ordinary frames comes from the tracker. A second, smaller model may emit a box only after verification has already declared a failure, and even those commits sometimes destroy a correct track.

### Cited Findings
- Dual-Agent (arXiv:2610.15929, 11 Oct 2026) freezes SAM 3 as the propagator. A verification agent (Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking, zero-shot) sees exemplar frames with the trusted box and context frames with the tracker’s box, and answers whether the tracker is still on the target. The prompt asks it to locate the instance and only then judge the red box. A correction agent (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, fine-tuned) runs only if verification says the track is wrong, and only then emits a visibility bit and a box in the Qwen normalized \([0,1000]^4\) format. The authors state the split explicitly: “deciding whether the track is wrong and deciding where the target is should not be treated as the same task.” — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- They refuse a per-frame language call. “Applying a VLM at every frame is both computationally expensive and potentially harmful, since unnecessary corrections can overwrite a valid tracker state.” Triggers are occlusion (poll every \(M\) frames), reappearance (delayed by at least \(N\) frames), and short-horizon variance of SAM 3’s confidence. If confidence is already above a threshold, verification is skipped. A commit requires the corrected box to have IoU below 0.5 with the tracker box, so a near-duplicate redraw does not enter memory. Verification can be repeated at \(t\), \(t+4\), and \(t+8\) and majority-voted. — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- Frequency, measured on SecVOS: 1,360 verifier calls, 240 corrector calls, 88 committed corrections. The verifier runs on 4.82% of unique frames. On 502 verifier decisions where a ground-truth box exists, verifier accuracy is 68.7%, precision 76.8%, recall 60.0%. Commits: 67 better, 19 worse, 2 neutral. Attribution at commit time: SAM 3 missed, 39 commits, IoU 0.000 → 0.856; drifted, 32 commits, 0.035 → 0.903; SAM 3 was already correct, 17 commits, 0.961 → 0.026. SecVOS overall \(\mathcal{J\&F}\) moves from 74.21 (SAM 3) to 76.94 (Dual-Agent), the +2.7 in the abstract. LVOS-v2 \(\mathcal{J\&F}\) moves from 88.2 to 89.4 (+1.2). The paper notes a slight drop on the seen subset, which it attributes to the verifier occasionally overruling a correct SAM 3 prediction. — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- Trigger ablation on SecVOS \(\mathcal{J\&F}\): occlusion polling alone 75.4; plus reappearance 75.6; plus confidence-variance trigger 76.94. SAM 3’s overall on that benchmark is 74.21, so occlusion polling alone is already most of the gain, and the variance trigger is the largest step after that. Voting ablation: window 3 and stride 4 gives 76.94; a single vote at stride 4 gives 76.8; window 3 and stride 2 gives 76.4. One correction round instead of three drops \(\mathcal{J\&F}\) from 76.94 to 75.6. — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- Cost versus polling rate, one H100: SAM 3 alone 12.2 seconds per video; full Dual-Agent 71.8 seconds at 76.94 \(\mathcal{J\&F}\); FP8 verification 56.2 seconds at 76.80; occlusion polling reduced to once every 24 frames, 47.7 seconds at 76.41. The paper says verification frequency and precision trade cost against accuracy, and that the system is not real-time. — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- The same controller, without retraining, improves SAM 2.1, SeC, and Cutie. Cutie exposes no confidence score, so that transfer uses only the occlusion-polling trigger. Gains are larger on weaker propagators. — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- Image-level, not video: the 2025 verification workflow uses the VLM only as a true/false judge of detector boxes. Organic GPT-4o Acc@0.5 is under 10% when it must produce the box. Verification-first GPT-4o is 81.2 / 76.3 / 75.9 on RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg. Contrastive Region Guidance (Wan et al., ECCV 2024) is the earlier pattern they compare against: a VLM re-scores boxes that Grounding DINO already drew. Their table lists Grounding DINO + CRG at 81.6 / 73.2 on the RefCOCO test columns, above the REC-trained Grounding DINO row in that same table. — [arXiv:2509.09958](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09958v2)
- ReTrackVLM (Applied Sciences, 12 Feb 2025) uses a vision-language embedding plus a zero-shot re-identification module and a Kalman filter. It does not draw boxes from language. On MOT15, MOT16, MOT17, MOT20, and DanceTrack it reports +1.5 MOTA and −10.3% identity switches versus the methods it compares, and 91.7% precision on MOT17, with identity switches rising again in very dense scenes. Those sets are pedestrian and dance tracking, not tabletop parts. — [Bayraktar, Appl. Sci. 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/4/1907)

### Inferences
- The published policy for “keep the box or drop the frame” is closer to Dual-Agent’s commit gate than to trusting the second locate call. A language box is allowed into the track only after a separate verdict that the track has failed, and only if the new box is substantially different (IoU under 0.5). Even then, 19 of 88 commits made the track worse, and 17 of those overwrote a correct box so badly that IoU fell from 0.961 to 0.026. A neighbor box from a locate call is in that harm set.
- Calling the language model on every sampled frame is the condition the paper says is harmful, not only costly. Sparse triggers (4.82% of frames) produced the +2.7 \(\mathcal{J\&F}\). Stretching occlusion polling to every 24 frames cost 0.53 \(\mathcal{J\&F}\) (76.94 to 76.41) and cut runtime from 71.8 s to 47.7 s per video.
- The corrector still emits coordinates, but only as a recovery specialist after failure is declared, and the propagator remains SAM 3. That is not “the language model is the per-frame box generator.”

### Gaps
- Dual-Agent’s \(M\), \(N\), confidence threshold, and variance threshold are selected on a held-out SA-V shard and not restated as a single recommended rate in the sections read here. The every-24-frames figure is the explicit frequency ablation.
- No ablation was found that compares “VLM draws the box every frame” against “VLM only verifies” on egocentric tabletop video.
- ReTrackVLM’s identity-switch reduction is on person-tracking benchmarks. It is not evidence about parts on a table.
- Dual-Agent verifier precision is 76.8% and recall is 60.0%. A reject decision is wrong often enough that an automatic drop of the frame will discard good frames. The paper does not convert that into a recommended drop rate.

## Which egocentric or tabletop pipelines keep object identity through hand occlusion without redrawing a language box on every frame?

### Takeaway
The pipelines that publish identity-through-occlusion numbers do not re-query a language model per frame. They propagate a mask or a 3D centroid from a first prompt, and they treat a later similar object as a known failure of 2D appearance matching. Language, when it appears at all, is a sparse check or a keyframe refresh, not the source of the box.

### Cited Findings
- EPIC-KITCHENS VISOR (Darkhalil et al., NeurIPS 2022, older) is the egocentric hand-and-object segmentation benchmark on EPIC-KITCHENS-100: 271K sparse masks over 36 hours, dense interpolations, 67K hand–object relations, 257 classes in the 2025 challenge report’s description. The video-object-segmentation task is to track the objects annotated on the first frame through later frames, including occlusion, disappearance, and reappearance. Annotation rules for partial occlusion say to segment only the visible parts of the occluded object, not to invent the hidden extent and not to merge it with the occluder. — [VISOR site](https://epic-kitchens.github.io/VISOR/site); [NeurIPS 2022 appendix](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/590a7ebe0da1f262c80d0188f5c4c222-Supplemental-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf); [2025 VOS challenge report](https://epic-kitchens.github.io/static/results2025/Reports/VOS-R2.pdf)
- A 2025 VISOR semi-supervised VOS entry fine-tunes SAM 2 and reports 87.5% \(\mathcal{J\&F}\) on the test set. The tracker is prompted from the first-frame objects and then propagated. The report does not describe a language model redrawing boxes. — [VOS-R2 report](https://epic-kitchens.github.io/static/results2025/Reports/VOS-R2.pdf)
- SAM 2 (Ravi et al., 2024) is the model those egocentric entries start from. Prompts are clicks, boxes, or masks on a frame, encoded as positional embeddings, not as a sentence. A memory bank holds a FIFO of up to \(N\) recent frame memories plus prompted-frame memories, and object pointers from the mask decoder. An occlusion head predicts whether the object is present. If the object is absent, a learned no-object embedding is added to the spatial memory so the memory is not filled with the occluder’s appearance. The first-frame memory is kept for the whole video in the standard semi-supervised setting. The paper’s interactive example is a single later click repairing a lost object, because memory lets one prompt recover the masklet; restarting segmentation from scratch would require a full re-annotation. SA-V, the training set, is described as containing small objects and parts that occlude and reappear. The paper reports video segmentation with 3× fewer interactions than prior approaches. It does not take a referring expression at each frame. — [arXiv:2408.00714](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)
- Bhalgat et al. (arXiv:2408.09860, 2024) track instances in egocentric video on EPIC Fields by combining scene geometry, 3D object-centroid tracking, and an initial 2D video-object-segmentation track. They describe the failure this is aimed at: a cook sets a pan down and later looks at other pans, and a 2D segmenter associates the lookalike with the original track; occlusion and leaving the frame make that worse. Versus DEVA, they report +77 association accuracy and +4.5 IDF1, and a 73% to 80% reduction in identity switches across categories. Mean identity switches per video fall from 27.21 to 5.29 for knives, 14.53 to 2.88 for taps, and 19.44 to 4.11 for pans. Identity is a 3D centroid carried through frames when the object is occluded or out of view, not a language box. — [arXiv:2408.09860](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09860v1)
- Dual-Agent, above, is not an egocentric-kitchen paper. Its benchmarks are LVOS-v2, SecVOS, LaSOT-ext, TNL2K, and VOT-LT2022. The mechanism is the one egocentric memory trackers were missing: SAM-style propagation already survives short occlusion, and the language model is a sparse verifier when memory has latched onto a distractor. The harmful commit (IoU 0.961 → 0.026) is what a per-frame language redraw risks doing to a track that was still correct through the hand. — [arXiv:2610.15929](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)
- The Grounded-SAM-2 repository’s continuous-identity demo, which is code rather than a benchmark paper, refreshes a SAM 2 propagation with occasional detector keyframes and matches detections to existing tracks by IoU (described as a default threshold of 0.8). A detection that does not meet the threshold becomes a new identity. That is detector boxes at keyframes plus mask propagation, not a language box every frame. The threshold is an implementation default, not a published accuracy. — [DeepWiki summary of the demo](https://deepwiki.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2/4.3-continuous-id-tracking)

### Inferences
- For a fixed camera or a head-mounted camera, the published way to hold a small object’s identity while a hand covers it is: prompt a segmenter once (box, mask, or point), propagate with memory that can say “not visible,” and keep a geometric identity (SAM 2 memory, or a 3D centroid on EPIC Fields) until the object is visible again. VISOR’s own rule is to store the visible fragment during partial occlusion, not a guessed full extent.
- Redrawing from language on the occluded frame fights that design. The occluder is the salient region, and GroundingME’s small-object and rejection numbers say the language box is likely to be a neighbor or an invented region. Dual-Agent’s 17 harmful commits show that writing such a region into memory is worse than leaving the gap.
- A reference crop used as a visual prompt (DINO-X / T-Rex2) is a different operation from asking a chat model to return a normalized box. The crop conditions a detection decoder. It does not make the language model a safe box generator.

### Gaps
- No Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS, HOI, Assembly101, or IKEA-assembly paper read here reports an identity-switch rate for a system that redraws boxes from a language model on every frame. The absence is the finding: the pipelines that publish occlusion identity numbers do not use that design.
- VISOR’s 87.5% \(\mathcal{J\&F}\) is one 2025 challenge report for fine-tuned SAM 2, not a comparison against a language-box baseline.
- Bhalgat et al. reduce identity switches on EPIC Fields using 3D geometry. They do not report a hand-occlusion-only subset, and the knife number (27.21 → 5.29) is a mean over videos for that class, not a per-occlusion rate.
- The Grounded-SAM-2 IoU 0.8 keyframe rule is repository documentation, not a paper.
- No sourced identity-switch number was found for tabletop manipulation parts under hand occlusion in 2025–2026 assembly-video papers during this pass.
