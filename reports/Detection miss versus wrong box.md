# Stop trading misses for wrong boxes

LearnLoop stops trading a missed visible part for a box on the wrong region by taking coordinate generation away from the vision-language model and storing a third outcome: a frame that was searched and left empty. The region on ordinary samples comes from a local, class-agnostic mask propagator prompted by a confirmed observation. Language, when a provider is configured, only judges whether that mask is still the same part, and the best published measurement calls that verifier on **4.82%** of frames. That split is the architecture the 2024–2026 localization and video-segmentation literature supports. Another overlap, size, or color cutoff is a different point on the same curve. A chat model redrawing a normalized box on every one-hertz sample is the policy those papers measure as harmful. An unmodified dense-frame tracker sits outside the short-gap regime its motion gate was measured in. On benchmarks built for small objects and lookalikes, coordinate-emitting general models land in the low tens of percent or near zero, and a second locate call that returns a neighbor is scored there as a positive detection of the distractor. The lab can build the propagator with released weights and no API key, keep refused regions visible only in diagnostics, and judge the change by false boxes, misses, and coverage counted apart.

## Language models select a neighbor and then draw its extent

RefCOCO-family scores above 90% describe a saturated, noisy, mostly large-object task. A manual audit found labeling-error rates of **14% on RefCOCO, 24% on RefCOCO+, and 5% on RefCOCOg**, and removing those items moved four large multimodal models by only **1.57 to 3.08 points**. CogVLM-Grounding sits at **92.44%** on the original RefCOCO benchmark and **94.58%** on the cleaned one, which the authors treat as saturation rather than as proof of fine localization ([Chen et al., Ref-L4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16866)). The sets built because those numbers had stopped meaning anything tell a different story. Ref-L4 uses expressions of **mean length 24.2 words** across **45,341** referring expressions. There GPT-4V scores **9.91% Acc@0.5, 1.19% at IoU 0.75, and 0.12% at IoU 0.9**. On small objects its Acc@0.5 is **2.13%**. The strongest box-emitting model in that table, CogVLM-Grounding, still falls from **86.43% on medium objects to 75.06% on small ones**, and only **48.35%** of its predictions reach IoU 0.9 ([Chen et al., Ref-L4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16866)).

GroundingME was built for the case the lab actually has: several lookalikes, and targets that occupy a fraction of a percent of a large image. Instance-area-ratio quartiles are **0.16%, 1.0%, and 2.7%**. Intra-class instance-count quartiles are **5, 7, and 12**. Under greedy decoding the best listed model, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B, reaches **45.1% Acc@0.5** overall, **72.7% on occlusion, 35.3% on small objects, and 0.0% on rejection**. Scaling a Qwen3-VL from 2B to 32B moves overall accuracy from **21.1% to 39.5%** and does not create a refusal. Thinking mode adds **4.7 to 7.4 points** on the models tested. The project leaderboard’s thinking run of Qwen3-VL-A22B is **49.8% overall and 5.5% rejection**, and the paper says thinking hurts perception-heavy subtasks relative to reasoning-heavy ones ([Li et al., GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1); [leaderboard](https://groundingme.github.io/)). The rejection subset is **201 of 1,005 samples, 20%** of the benchmark, and it asks the model to emit no box. Most of 25 models emit one anyway. Training that adds negative samples raises rejection from **0% to 27.9%** for a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B. That is a training change. A prompt that asks an off-the-shelf model to abstain faces the measured rejection rate of zero ([Li et al., GroundingME](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17495v1)). SOREC pushes size further. Every target occupies **less than 1% of the image**, and a typical box is about **0.05%**. Zero-shot MM-GroundingDINO scores **0.6 / 1.0 / 0.2 Acc@0.5** on the SOREC splits, against **50.4 / 57.2 / 43.2** zero-shot and **89.2 / 91.9 / 86.0** fully fine-tuned on RefCOCO. Full fine-tuning on SOREC’s large split still reaches only **63.7 / 69.6 / 55.6**. The transferable fact is the size regime. SOREC is driving imagery, and the categories do not transfer to a tabletop ([Goto et al., SOREC](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03701)).

Scored as a detector, the same class of model still emits regions that are not objects. Prompted for every COCO category, Qwen3-VL-8B’s raw boxes score **20.43 AP**, against **42.0 AP** for an older supervised DETR-R50. Of **66,590** boxes, **68.1%** match no ground-truth object of that class at IoU 0.5. Across that paper’s four localization benchmarks, **58% to 68%** of emitted regions are not valid objects or events. Coordinates are written digit by digit on a **0 to 1000** grid, and once generation has committed, the token probabilities become nearly deterministic, so they barely separate a grounded box from a hallucinated one. Re-ranking with an attention score lifts the same model to **32.12 AP** at one sample and **37.01 AP** at 16 samples, still short of the DETR ([Propose and Attend](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2607.05978)). A sharper score on a coordinate string is still a score on the wrong generator. The lab’s model scores are in this category. They are ranks at best, and for coordinate tokens they collapse once the box has been written.

The systems that hold up on hard referring sets, and on open-vocabulary detection, never ask an autoregressive chat model to write the box. Grounding DINO selects image tokens by similarity to the text, **top 900 by maximum similarity**, then a decoder regresses boxes with **L1 and GIoU** losses. The language link is a contrastive score. For referring expressions the paper’s rule is to take the highest-scoring box ([Liu et al., Grounding DINO](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.05499v5)). Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro keeps that design and reports **54.3 COCO zero-shot AP, 55.7 LVIS-minival, and 47.6 LVIS-val** ([Grounding DINO 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.10300v2)). DINO-X uses the same box head, an MLP on decoder queries, and adds a **visual prompt** — a box or a point on the desired instance — for the cases where text falls short because the thing is rare or hard to describe. Its zero-shot box AP is **56.0 on COCO, 59.8 on LVIS-minival, and 52.4 on LVIS-val** ([DINO-X](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.14347v1)). Those are category-prompt detection numbers, and several older training sets overlap the evaluation images. They are evidence about which module emits coordinates. They are not a promise about unnamed parts well under one percent of the frame. A shared text embedding still cannot decide which of two similar red parts is the tracked one. That remaining failure is instance ranking after boxes exist, which is why a reference crop belongs on a detection decoder or a verifier.

Two further designs make the same cut. Groma states that localization “requires little semantic understanding but demands perceptual skills, which is typically out of the scope of an LLM’s expertise.” A class-agnostic detector proposes up to 300 regions, and the language model refers to tokens already anchored to those regions. On LVIS-Ground, average recall is **4.9 for Shikra**, with small-object AR **0.1**, **16.8 for Ferret**, and **28.8 for Groma** ([Ma et al., Groma](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13013)). ChatRex feeds proposal boxes into the language model and trains it to emit **indices**, citing Qwen2-VL’s **43.9 COCO recall** when the language model predicts coordinates itself ([Jiang et al., ChatRex](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18363)). An image-level verification workflow makes the gap numerical. Asked to produce the box, GPT-4o scores **under 10% Acc@0.5** on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg. Asked only true or false about boxes from a detector, the same model reaches **81.2 / 76.3 / 75.9**. Judging one box at a time, with the proposals held fixed, beats a single selection prompt by about **6 to 10 points** on most splits and by **18.0 points** on average across three benchmarks and two models ([Verification-first REC](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09958v2)). A 2026 comparison finds the same reversal on the hard set and the opposite ranking on the easy one. On Ref-L4 val, MQADet with Grounding DINO scores **59.35 Acc@0.5** against **18.55** for Qwen2-VL-2B, while on RefCOCO testA the small grounding model scores **76.99** against **60.03** for that detector pipeline ([Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-36936-x/tables/4)). Florence-2 is the sequence model in this family that does emit quantized location tokens, because it was trained as a grounder, which is a different object from a general chat model asked for a JSON box ([Florence-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06242); [architecture notes](https://github.com/anyantudre/Florence-2-Vision-Language-Model)). In the grounded-segmentation code path, a detector proposes the box and a segmenter consumes it. The mask decoder never receives the referring sentence ([Grounded-SAM-2](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/Grounded-SAM-2/)).

## Propagated masks keep identity where one-hertz motion gates fail

The video systems that publish identity through occlusion do not re-query a language model for a box on every frame. SAM 2 takes a point, a box, or a mask on any frame, encodes it as a positional prompt, and streams a masklet. An image encoder runs once per frame, memory attention conditions that embedding on a bank of recent-frame memories plus prompted-frame memories, and a mask decoder emits the mask. An occlusion head can declare the object absent, and an absent frame is stored with a learned occlusion embedding so the occluder’s appearance does not fill the memory. Objects are decoded independently and do not exclude one another ([Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)). XMem and Cutie, in the standard protocol, start from a first-frame mask and read later frames out of memory. Cutie adds object-level queries on top of pixel memory because independent pixel matching has no object consistency and locks onto distractors ([Cheng and Schwing, XMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07115); [Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)). DEVA separates the jobs in the way the lab needs. A task-specific image segmenter proposes masks. A class-agnostic propagator carries them. A new image mask joins an existing track only when the two agree, and an unmatched proposal passes through as its own segment. A propagated object survives image-model misses and is deleted only after repeated failed associations. In their default the image model is not called every frame ([Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)).

On the paper’s first-frame-mask table, SAM 2 Hiera-L reaches **77.9 J&F on MOSE val, 90.7 on DAVIS 2017 val, and 78.4 on SA-V test**, while XMem, DEVA, and Cutie cluster near **60 to 63 on SA-V**. The later GitHub checkpoint card reports different absolute numbers, including **79.5** SA-V test and **74.6** MOSE val for SAM 2.1 Hiera-L, so the ordering is the claim and the tables are not interchangeable ([Ravi et al., SAM 2, Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2); [SAM 2 checkpoints](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2/)). The wrong-object failure is documented, and no fetched paper gives a rate such as the percent of masks that landed on the neighbor. SAM 2 names crowds, long occlusions, long videos, thin fast detail, and nearby objects of similar appearance. The authors’ example is multiple identical juggling balls, and the mitigation they name is another prompt on a later frame ([Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2); [Meta, Segment Anything 2](https://ai.meta.com/blog/segment-anything-2/)). SAM2Long identifies the mechanism that turns one bad frame into a track. The decoder emits three masks and an occlusion score. Default inference keeps only the highest predicted-IoU mask. On occlusion or reappearance that uncertain mask is written into the memory FIFO, and later frames attend to it. Their training-free fix keeps several pathways, refuses to store frames the occlusion score or the predicted IoU marks as absent or poorly segmented, and forces distinct masks when every pathway is uncertain. Relative to their own SAM 2 reproduction, the average gain across the comparisons they summarize is **3.7 J&F**, and the large model gains **5.3** on SA-V test. Those deltas are the usable result. Absolute scores disagree across SAM 2 tables ([Ding et al., SAM2Long](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16268v3)).

Cutie’s older measurement of the same neighbor failure is the drop from easy video to crowded, occluded video. Methods they cite lose **more than 20 J&F** from DAVIS 2017 to MOSE. In their table, XMem trained without MOSE falls from **86.2 to 56.3**, a **29.9 point** drop, and Cutie-base reaches **64.0**, the abstract’s **+8.7**. With MOSE in training the gap shrinks and remains: **68.3 versus 59.6** ([Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)). No paper read for this report gives a numeric rate of masks placed on a hand, on a same-color neighbor, or on a tabletop part. Where hands appear, they are occluders. VISOR’s annotation rule for partial occlusion is to store the visible fragment, and to avoid inventing the hidden extent or merging it with the occluder ([VISOR](https://epic-kitchens.github.io/VISOR/site)). EgoHOS segments the hand and removes it so the object behind it can be seen ([Zhang et al., EgoHOS](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136890125.pdf)). A 2025 VISOR entry fine-tunes SAM 2 from the first-frame objects and reports **87.5% J&F**, with no language model redrawing boxes ([VOS-R2](https://epic-kitchens.github.io/static/results2025/Reports/VOS-R2.pdf)). On EPIC Fields, a 2D segmenter associates a lookalike pan with the original track once the first pan is set down. A 3D centroid carried through occlusion cuts identity switches by **73% to 80%** across categories. Reported mean switches per video fall from **27.21 to 5.29 for knives, 14.53 to 2.88 for taps, and 19.44 to 4.11 for pans** ([Bhalgat et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.09860v1)). That is the published upgrade when unconstrained head motion and lookalikes defeat 2D appearance. It is a geometric identity. Whether a carried mask should be shown through a hand, as amodal completion, or withheld by an occlusion head, is unsettled by any head-to-head number on parts.

The language model’s published role on top of this propagator is a sparse verdict, issued after the mask already exists. Dual-Agent freezes SAM 3 as the carrier. A verification model sees exemplar frames and the tracker’s box and answers whether the track is still the target. A correction model runs only if verification says the track is wrong, and only then emits a box. The authors’ split is explicit: deciding that the track is wrong and deciding where the target is are different tasks. They refuse a per-frame language call because an unnecessary correction overwrites a valid tracker state and the error is then propagated. On SecVOS the verifier runs on **4.82% of unique frames** (**1,360** verifier calls, **240** corrector calls, **88** commits). Where a ground-truth box exists, verifier accuracy is **68.7%**, precision **76.8%**, and recall **60.0%**, on **502** decisions. Of the commits, **67** improved the track, **19** made it worse, and **2** were neutral. **Seventeen** commits overwrote a correct mask, taking IoU from **0.961 to 0.026**. Overall J&F moves from **74.21 to 76.94** on SecVOS and from **88.2 to 89.4** on LVOS-v2. Stretching occlusion polling to once every 24 frames costs **0.53 J&F**, from **76.94 to 76.41**. The same controller, without retraining, also improves weaker propagators ([Bundele et al., Dual-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15929)). A neighbor box from a locate call sits in the harm set those seventeen commits measured. Precision of **76.8%** means about **23%** of “track is wrong” verdicts are themselves wrong, so an automatic drop discards good frames, and recall of **60%** means four in ten real failures are missed. The honest handling of a reject is an abstention with a coverage count, stored so it can be audited. The product view shows a gap. The research view keeps the rejected region as a hypothesis.

What the literature does not support is taking ByteTrack, BoT-SORT, or OC-SORT as published and running them on one-hertz samples. Those trackers predict a location, refuse the match when overlap or direction fails, and then withhold the track. A lost track is not drawn. A low-score detection cannot birth an identity. ByteTrack’s own low-frame-rate result is the relevant measurement. On BDD100K, which the authors describe as large camera motion and low frame rate, IoU association scores **39.4 mMOTA and 27,902 identity switches**, against **45.5 mMOTA and 9,140 identity switches** when appearance leads the first association. Their rule is that location and motion are for short-range matching, and appearance is for re-identification after a long occlusion. Using appearance on the low-score, occluded second stage hurts ([Zhang et al., ByteTrack](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.06864v3)). BoT-SORT shows why head motion breaks the overlap assumption. Camera motion moves the image, the predicted box no longer overlaps the detection, and the failure is an identity switch or a false negative. They compensate with a global image transform before association. Appearance, updated only from high-confidence crops, barely moves detection: IoU alone is **78.4 MOTA** and the fused cost is **78.5** on their MOT17 validation ablation. They also report that vibration and wind-driven drift move static-camera sequences, with MOT20 as the example. Linear interpolation of gaps is a post-process with a maximum of 20 frames, reported apart from association ([Aharon et al., BoT-SORT](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14651v2)). OC-SORT’s derivation is sharper. During occlusion the filter predicts with no measurement, and if per-frame position noise is on the order of the inter-frame displacement, a **10-frame** occlusion can move the predicted center by about the object size. They call the linear model acceptable only for very short occlusions. On DanceTrack, where appearance is similar and localization is comparatively easy (**DetA 71.6 for ByteTrack, 80.4 for OC-SORT**), association accuracy is the failure: **31.4 versus 40.2**. Interpolation is again its own row ([Cao et al., OC-SORT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.14360v2)).

That ten-frame analysis is at video rate. A one-second hole at 30 frames per second is thirty frames, three times the occlusion OC-SORT treats as unsafe, so a tracker that interpolates motion across one missed one-hertz sample is outside the regime they call safe. No source in these notes measures ByteTrack, BoT-SORT, or OC-SORT on a one-hertz parts video, so there is no parts number to import. There is a published mechanism, and it is silence after a failed match. The same honesty applies to the propagators. No evaluation here runs SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, or DEVA when the only frames are one second apart. SA-V’s hard targets were annotated at **6 fps**, and YouTube-VOS is annotated at 6 fps on 30 fps video ([Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2); [Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)). A one-hertz transfer is unmeasured. The architecture to carry across that gap is the occlusion outcome and the refusal to write an uncertain mask into memory. The J&F figures above are not a forecast of the lab’s one-hertz accuracy.

Open-vocabulary multi-object tracking does not close the unnamed-part problem either. OVTrack can take class names at test time that were absent from training. On TAO novel classes it scores **LocA 48.8, AssocA 33.6, and ClsA 1.5**. Adding RegionCLIP raises novel classification accuracy to **11.4** and leaves association essentially unchanged at **33.2**. The authors distinguish this from open-world tracking, where the class set is unknown. Novel classes here are LVIS rare classes, a proxy that still has names ([Li et al., OVTrack](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Li_OVTrack_Open-Vocabulary_Multiple_Object_Tracking_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)). Unfamiliar physical parts, with no name list at all, are outside that proxy. The matching front-end is a class-agnostic proposer.

## Count false boxes, misses, and coverage as separate events

A single confidence, geometric or linguistic, picks one point on a curve. Geifman and El-Yaniv define a selective classifier as a pair: a predictor and a gate. When the gate is off, the output is don’t know. Coverage is the fraction of cases the gate lets through. Selective risk is the error only on that fraction. Stricter risk lowers coverage. On ImageNet top-5, **2% error** can be guaranteed with probability **99.9%** at almost **60% coverage**. The score is required only to rank. It is not required to be a probability ([Geifman and El-Yaniv](https://arxiv.org/html/1705.08500v2)). Conformal prediction uses the same discipline. Softmax values enter as inputs to a calibration quantile. Under exchangeability, a calibration set of size n and a user error rate α give a set that contains the true label with probability between **1−α and 1−α+1/(n+1)** ([Angelopoulos and Bates](https://www.emerald.com/ftmal/article/16/4/494/1332423/Conformal-Prediction-A-Gentle-Introduction)). Platt scaling has no such finite-sample guarantee ([Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14193)). Temperature scaling improves vision-language calibration empirically, including under some domain shifts, and it remains an empirical fit ([Tu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/tu24a.html)).

Detection is where that guarantee breaks for the output the lab emits today. Split conformal abstention on CLIPSeg meets the nominal level: at α of 0.05, pixel coverage is **95.14%** in normal weather and **95.45%** in adverse weather. The same procedure on Grounding DINO stays systematically below the target. The best Monte Carlo dropout baseline falls by nearly **10%**. At α of 0.10 under normal weather, the hybrid strategy reaches **69.47% coverage** against a **90%** nominal level, and the detection risk-coverage curves remain flat ([Liu and Mohanty](https://huazeliu.github.io/files/paper/ICCV_T2FM.pdf)). Box-interval conformal prediction has a second limit that matches the miss. The guarantee holds for objects the detector already emitted, and it does not account for undetected objects. Distribution-free coverage conditional on the particular image is impossible. The calibrated quantity is a geometric residual around an emitted box, not a probability that the box is the right instance ([Timans et al.](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/12292.pdf)). Closed-ended visual question answering can be held to a user error rate when the answer is a choice among known labels and calibration labels exist ([Ye and Wei](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.17671)). No source in these notes proves a coverage guarantee for a vision-language box when the calibration set has no label for the unfamiliar part. Exchangeability is also the assumption video threatens. Adverse weather already moves detection coverage in that KITTI study, and no paper here measures temporal dependence. The lab’s scores can be plotted as risk against coverage on a held-out diagnostic slice drawn like the test video. They stay uncalibrated ranks. They do not become probabilities by being thresholded.

The geometric and color checks the lab already runs are resemblance ranks of this kind. OLN’s objectness is centerness and predicted overlap, a localization quality, and the paper’s result is that a foreground classifier learns to call unlabeled objects background. Predicting that quality is what moves novel-category recall. Regressing a binary target on the same classifier head does not ([Kim et al., OLN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.06753v1)). A geometric residual can demote a region from committed identity to searched-and-empty. It cannot certify a different region as the part, and Timans’ intervals explicitly exclude misses. Retuning overlap, size, or color selects another point on Geifman’s curve. The second output the open-world detectors added is a different object, not a stricter cutoff. PROB multiplies a density-based objectness into the class distribution so a near-zero objectness suppresses every name. Disabling that term makes objectness random and cuts known-class accuracy ([Zohar et al., PROB](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Zohar_PROB_Probabilistic_Objectness_for_Open_World_Object_Detection_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)). CAT localizes in one decoder pass and identifies in a second. Removing the cascade drops unknown recall by **2.0 to 2.6 points** ([Ma et al., CAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.01970v3)). SAM returns a valid mask and a predicted mask quality, and its data engine stored no names. An ambiguous prompt must yield a reasonable mask for at least one object, which is existence without an identity ([Kirillov et al., SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02643)).

The unknown class in open-world detection is a third, easy-to-confuse state. It is a committed detection that refuses a known name. Wilderness impact and absolute open-set error penalize an unknown that was called a known class, and they do not punish silence. Unknown recall was added because those two numbers never measured retrieval. Zhao and colleagues then split retrieval itself. Their localization rate counts a box on the unknown even when the name is wrong. Their discrimination rate asks whether that box was actually labeled unknown. On their rebuilt benchmark the ORE row has unknown recall **7.76**, localization **20.39**, and discrimination **38.04**: the box landed, and on about **62%** of those localized unknowns the name was still wrong ([Joseph et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02603v2); [Zhao et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.00471v2)). A system that drops every doubtful box can improve the silence-friendly metrics while retrieval falls. That is the lab’s present tradeoff. Hiding the uncertain box improves the appearance of precision. Showing it accepts a region that the rejection benchmarks score as the distractor.

Average precision hides the same tradeoff inside one curve. A fixed overlap, conventionally 0.5, turns each detection into a binary success before the score threshold ever acts, and average precision then averages precision along recall. Hall and colleagues’ illustration is an airplane box covering **16% of the object’s pixels**: a complete true positive under average precision at IoU 0.5, and a pairwise PDQ of **3.64×10⁻⁶** if the detector reports no spatial uncertainty. PDQ’s denominator still counts the unmatched detection and the missed ground truth, and neither contributes to the numerator ([Hall et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_WACV_2020/papers/Hall_Probabilistic_Object_Detection_Definition_and_Evaluation_WACV_2020_paper.pdf)). HOTA separates detection, association, and localization. At a given overlap threshold, detection accuracy is the Jaccard index **|TP| / (|TP| + |FN| + |FP|)**. A committed box that misses the threshold adds a false positive and leaves the ground truth as a false negative. Dropping that box removes the false positive and leaves the false negative. The wrong box is the more expensive event, and a single operating point does not show the two counts. HOTA then averages across localization thresholds, with a separate localization accuracy on the true positives only ([Luiten et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11263-020-01375-2)).

TETA refuses to score a wrong name as a missed object. Localization is matched by place, inside a local cluster. Association and classification are scored on well-localized matches, and association does not require the class to agree. The three terms are averaged arithmetically so a zero classification score does not wipe out a correct track. Their motivating clip is the collapse the lab should refuse to copy: a tracker that follows the object and misses a fine-grained class scores **0 mMOTA**, and a tracker that names the class and does not track scores **33** ([Li et al., TETA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820486.pdf)). For LearnLoop, a box on the wrong area is a localization false positive and a localization false negative. A box on the part with the wrong identity moves the classification or association term. A withheld box lowers localization recall and creates no false positive. HOTA and class-grouped average precision fold the first two into a miss.

One caveat decides whether the diagnostic set is worth building. If labels are incomplete, TETA ignores predictions that fall in no cluster, because large-vocabulary sets are not exhaustive and counting every extra box as a false positive punishes objects nobody boxed. DEVA’s BURST numbers carry the same warning. False-positive tracks are not directly penalized, and the uncommon-class gain for a class-agnostic front-end is real under that rule: **uncommon open-world tracking accuracy 50.5** for EntitySeg plus DEVA, against **41.5** for a COCO-class segmenter on the same propagator and **24.0** for Mask2Former plus a box tracker ([Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)). A wrong-area box on a part that was never annotated would go unpunished. PDQ and HOTA, which treat every unmatched prediction as a false positive, punish it. The honest set for this tradeoff is exhaustively labeled. On that set the report is four quantities per identity, not one threshold sweep: localization Jaccard across overlap thresholds, localization quality of the matches, association, and coverage of the frames where the system abstained. Stratify by fixed camera, constrained head-mounted, and unconstrained head-mounted, and within those by small area, occlusion, and lookalikes. GroundingME’s best no-thinking model is at **72.7% on occlusion and 35.3% on small objects**. Those are different failures. A partly visible but tiny part is closer to the small subset than to the occlusion subset. Interpolation keeps the separate row BoT-SORT and OC-SORT already give it, and it stays out of the detection counts.

| Frame record | What the lab stores | What the score does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committed mask on the part | The propagator’s mask, with memory updated | Localization true positive; association scored on this row |
| Committed region on a neighbor, the hand, or empty space | A wrong area written into the identity | False positive and false negative together; later samples inherit the error if memory was updated |
| Searched, nothing committed | Occlusion, non-match, or verifier abstention; the refused region kept in diagnostics | False negative, plus a coverage loss; no false positive |
| Right place, wrong identity | Mask on the part, track id or name wrong | Association or classification error; localization can still be right |
| Interpolated gap | A post-process between real observations | Its own row, excluded from detection counts |

## Confirm locally, propagate every sample, verify after failure

The build that matches the evidence treats a missing API key as a legal configuration. SAM 2, XMem, Cutie, and DEVA’s propagator are class-agnostic, and the weights and code are public. On one A100 the SAM 2 paper measures Hiera-B+ at **43.8 FPS** and Hiera-L at **30.2 FPS**. The SAM 2.1 checkpoint card measures the large model at **39.5 FPS** and **224.4M** parameters on an A100. A 2025 measurement on 1080p LVOS v2 videos with an A40 is the conservative local figure: SAM 2.1 Large at **13.13 FPS and 5499 MB**, Base+ at **20.11 FPS and 4449 MB**, Tiny at **30.21 FPS and 4165 MB**. XMem’s long-video supplement sits near **1.2 to 1.5 GB**, at **23.4 FPS** for the larger memory cap on a V100. Cutie reports a similar running time to XMem and **1.35 to 2.36 GB** depending on the memory variant. No official CPU frames-per-second figure was found, and no source here prices a vision-language locate call, so a numerical cost ratio would be invented. The cost shape is still the one the lab can use. A one-hertz stream is one image encoding per second of video. Decoder and memory work scale with object count, because SAM 2 decodes objects separately ([Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2); [SAM 2 checkpoints](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2/); [SAM2Plus measurements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12252479/); [XMem results](https://github.com/hkchengrex/XMem/blob/main/docs/RESULTS.md); [Cheng et al., Cutie](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.12982v2)).

### A confirmed region becomes a mask

The first trusted observation of a part, whether a human confirmation or a local proposal the lab already computes, is converted once into a mask and written into propagator memory. A box is a legal SAM 2 prompt. XMem and Cutie want the mask. From that moment the identity is the memory, including the occlusion embedding, and normalized coordinates from a language model are off the path. Parts and subparts are inside this contract. SAM’s data collection stored masks with no category list, and SA-V is described as containing small parts that occlude and reappear, with **more than 88% of masks under a normalized area of 0.1** and a disappearance-and-reappearance rate of **42.5%** on SA-V Manual ([Kirillov et al., SAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02643); [Ravi et al., SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00714v2)).

### The next sample is a readout or an empty searched frame

Each later one-hertz sample is a memory readout. If the occlusion head marks the part absent, or the frame is one SAM2Long would refuse to store because the mask quality is poor, the identity’s record for that sample is empty and the frame is marked searched. Appearance memory is not updated from that sample. The visible fragment is kept when the propagator is on the part, following VISOR’s rule to store what is visible and to leave the hidden extent and the hand unpainted. If the only region on offer is a neighbor, the record stays empty. The refused region remains in the diagnostic log, attached to the identity as a hypothesis that was not committed, so a researcher can see the wrong area. The product surface shows the gap. The lab’s existing short-gap interpolation stays a display post-process. It carries the separate flag BoT-SORT and OC-SORT already isolate. It does not update memory, and it does not count as a detection. The one-hertz behavior of this readout is the measurement the lab has to make. Published J&F is not a substitute. DEVA’s habit of calling the image model every few video-rate frames does not transfer as a frame count. At one hertz, discovery runs when there is no track, and again when the occlusion head or the verifier declares a failure. The cadence is whatever the diagnostic slice supports.

### A new proposal opens its own track

The lab’s existing local proposals, or a class-agnostic point-grid segmenter of the kind DEVA already plugs in, stay the discovery front-end. DEVA’s published association merges an image mask into a carried mask when they agree, and otherwise lets the image mask through as a new segment. Lone proposals can be treated as noise by a short in-clip consensus. A miss does not delete the carried object. On BURST, the class-agnostic EntitySeg front-end is what raises uncommon-class open-world tracking accuracy, to **50.5** against **41.5** for a COCO-class segmenter on DEVA and **24.0** for Mask2Former plus a box tracker ([Cheng et al., DEVA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Cheng_Tracking_Anything_with_Decoupled_Video_Segmentation_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf); [DEVA repository](https://github.com/hkchengrex/Tracking-Anything-with-DEVA)). The mechanism to copy is the non-match outcome. A proposal that does not agree with the carried mask starts a candidate identity or is dropped as noise, and it does not repair the old identity. DEVA’s overlap rule and the MOT gates are those papers’ operating points on their data. They are not a parts threshold for LearnLoop, and they are not a reason to add another overlap, size, or color cutoff on the vision-language box. SAM 2’s independent decoding can still lay two similar parts on the same neighborhood. Diagnostics should show that collision. The authors’ repair for a confused masklet is a later prompt, which returns the system to the first step.

### Language answers whether the track has failed

When a provider is configured, it implements Dual-Agent’s verifier and nothing wider. The call is triggered by occlusion, reappearance, or unstable mask quality, and it is skipped when the propagator is already confident. The published operating point touched **4.82%** of frames and produced **+2.7 J&F**, with **19 of 88** commits harmful. The verifier receives the exemplar crop and the carried region and returns yes or no. A reference crop used this way is the visual prompt DINO-X adds when words cannot separate instances, and it is the exemplar Dual-Agent shows the verifier. A correction model runs only after a negative verdict. Its box is logged as a hypothesis. It updates memory only through a commit that diagnostics can reverse, because the measured failure mode is an unnecessary edit that replaces a correct mask with a disjoint one, IoU **0.961 to 0.026**. Verifier precision **76.8%** and recall **60.0%** are why a “no” becomes an abstention on the product surface and a retained hypothesis in the research record. With no API key this stage is absent. Propagation, abstention, and local discovery still run.

A locate call kept for a part that has no track yet returns a hypothesis from a box head, or a true-or-false judgment over regions that already exist. Groma, ChatRex, and the verification-first workflow are that design. A general model writing coordinates remains the component whose small-object Acc@0.5 is **2.13%** on Ref-L4, whose COCO boxes are **68.1%** unmatched, and whose GroundingME rejection is **zero** for most off-the-shelf systems. Optional appearance embeddings stay a cue for re-association after a long gap, which is the role ByteTrack assigns them, and they stay off occluded and abstained crops, which is BoT-SORT’s update rule. Cutie’s distractor result says pixel appearance alone is how a neighbor wins. An embedding is not the identity. On the unconstrained head-mounted set, if the one-hertz diagnostic shows the 2D memory swapping lookalikes, the published next component is a geometric identity of the kind Bhalgat carried on EPIC Fields. That measurement comes first. The fixed camera is the stratum closest to the static-camera regime, with BoT-SORT’s vibration caveat still in force. The constrained head-mounted camera is the middle stratum of the same diagnostic, not a third architecture.

### The diagnostic slice is the experiment the papers skipped

Before any further change to ranking, label an exhaustive slice in each camera regime. Incomplete labels would hide a wrong box on an unannotated part. For every identity and sample, record a localization true positive, a false positive, a false negative, and a coverage bit for abstention. Summarize with HOTA’s detection Jaccard across localization thresholds, localization accuracy of the matches, and TETA’s localization, association, and classification. Plot selective risk against coverage on the committed subset, using mask-quality scores and any language score as ranks, on a held-out slice drawn like the test video. Report identity switches on their own, especially under head motion. Report the one-hertz propagator on its own row, because no SAM 2, Cutie, XMem, or DEVA number in this literature was measured at a one-second interval. The permanent diagnostic record holds the carried mask or the empty state, any refused proposal, whether language was asked and what it answered, and whether memory was updated. That log is how a missed visible part stays visible as a gap, and how a box on the neighboring part stays visible as a rejected hypothesis.

## Conclusion

The uncertain box was a second object that won a selection, and storing it teaches the next sample the wrong appearance. A searched-and-empty frame ends that lesson. Precision and recall then belong to different modules: the propagator is judged by whether the mask stays on the part across a one-second gap, abstention is judged by coverage against the misses it creates, and the language model is judged by how rarely it is allowed to edit memory and by how often those edits are reversed. The number nobody has published is that split on unfamiliar parts at one sample per second, under a camera that moves with the head. If two-dimensional memory holds there, the lab already has the detector this literature describes. If it swaps lookalikes, identity moves into geometry, and the coordinate path still stays off the chat model.
